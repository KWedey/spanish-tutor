# Soft G (Before E and I)

## Phase
Phase C — introduced once learners have stable vowel production and encounter intermediate vocabulary containing gente, general, Argentina.

## The Sound

Before **e** or **i**, the letter G produces a fricative sound identical to J — either /x/ (uvular fricative, Castilian) or /h/ (glottal aspirate, Latin American). Before any other letter, G is a hard stop /g/ as in English "go."

| Context | IPA | Written Example |
|---------|-----|-----------------|
| G before e | /x/ or /h/ | gente, general, gerente |
| G before i | /x/ or /h/ | girar, girasol, gitano |
| G before a, o, u (or consonant) | /g/ | gato, goma, gusto, grande |

The soft G and the J (jota) are **the same sound**. "Gente" and "jefe" use identical phonemes. The spelling differs; the mouth position does not.

To preserve the hard /g/ sound before **e** or **i**, Spanish inserts a silent U: guerra, guitarra, guiño. The U is silent — its only job is to signal "this G is hard."

## Common English Speaker Errors

**Using the English hard G in soft-G contexts.** English "g" before "e" or "i" is soft in English too (gem, gin), but English soft G = /dʒ/ as in "judge." Spanish soft G = /x/ or /h/. An English speaker says "gente" with the front-of-mouth /dʒ/ sound; Spanish requires the back-of-throat /x/ or simple /h/.

**Treating GU as two sounds.** In "guerra" or "guitarra," learners sometimes pronounce the U. It is silent. The gu- cluster = hard /g/ only.

**Inconsistent application.** Hard G in Spanish is easy and frequent (gato, grande, gordo), so learners get comfortable with /g/ and then forget to switch before e/i.

## Minimal Pairs

| Hard G /g/ | Soft G /x/ or /h/ | Notes |
|-----------|-------------------|-------|
| gato | gente | cat / people |
| gusto | girar | taste / to turn |
| goma | general | rubber/eraser / general |
| gol | gitano | goal / gypsy |
| gordo | gerente | fat / manager |

Also contrast GU (hard) vs G-before-e/i (soft): guerra vs gente, guitarra vs girar.

## Practice Assignments

**Speechling recordings:**
- Record each soft-G word: `gente`, `general`, `girar`, `Argentina`, `gerente`, `gitano`, `gimnasio`, `geografía`, `girasol`
- Record minimal pair sets: `gato–gente`, `goma–general`, `gusto–girar`
- Record a sentence: `La gente de Argentina es muy generosa.`

**Forvo listening:**
- Listen to native pronunciations: `gente`, `general`, `Argentina`, `girar`, `gitano`, `gerente`, `geografía`
- Focus: is the G identical to a J in your target dialect? Can you hear the difference between hard G (gato) and soft G (gente)?

**Self-narration target words:**
Flag soft-G words encountered in daily narration: `gente`, `general`, `Argentina`, `gimnasio`, `geografía`. Consciously apply the /x/ or /h/ sound each time.

## Signs of Acquisition
- Speechling coach confirms soft-G words (gente, girar) are not mistaken for hard-G productions.
- Whisper transcription distinguishes gente/gentle-sounding words correctly without correction.
- Learner applies the soft G automatically before e/i without pausing to think about the rule.
- Correctly reads aloud unfamiliar G-words (e.g., geometría, gestión) using the right variant.
