# Pronunciation Practice

## Purpose

Framework for in-session pronunciation work when issues surface during a session. Pronunciation is
primarily delegated to external tools (Speechling for production practice, Forvo for reference
audio), but the tutor needs structured activities for real-time correction, targeted drills, and
building phonological awareness. These activities are reactive — triggered by observed errors — not
scheduled as standalone lessons.

## Phase Suitability

| Activity | Phase A | Phase B | Phase C | Phase D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimal Pair Drills | A+ | Yes | Yes | Rarely |
| Vowel Purity Check | Yes | Yes | Rarely | No |
| Stress Pattern Practice | A+ | Yes | Yes | Rarely |
| Connected Speech Shadowing | No | B+ | Yes | Yes |
| Sound-Spelling Correspondence | No | B+ | Yes | Yes |
| Intonation Contour Practice | No | No | C+ | Yes |

## Integration Guidance

Pronunciation work is inserted into a session, not planned as the main lesson. Use it:
- **During warm-up:** 2-3 minutes of minimal pairs or vowel drills as a physical warm-up.
- **After an error:** When a pronunciation issue blocks comprehension or recurs across sessions,
  pause and drill it for 2-4 minutes, then return to the lesson.
- **As a break:** Between grammar-heavy segments, a quick stress or shadowing drill resets energy.
- **Never more than 8 minutes total** of pronunciation work in a standard session.

## What NOT to Do

- Don't correct every pronunciation error. Focus on sounds that impede comprehension or are
  fossilizing. Let accent-level variation pass.
- Don't drill sounds outside the current phase focus. A Phase A learner doesn't need intonation
  contour work.
- Don't turn pronunciation into a test. Frame it as physical practice ("let's warm up the mouth").
- Don't spend more than 4 minutes on a single activity type. Switch or return to the lesson.

## Activity 1: Minimal Pair Drills

**When to use:** Phase A+. Learner confuses two phonemes (e.g., rolled vs tapped r, b/v merging).
Triggered by a production or perception error during conversation.

**How to run:**
1. Present 4-6 pairs. Say both words clearly: "pero / perro."
2. Perception check: say one word, learner identifies which. Do 4 rounds.
3. Production: learner says both words in each pair. Provide immediate feedback.
4. Context sentence: learner uses one word from each pair in a short sentence.

**Example items:**
- pero / perro, caro / carro, para / parra
- casa / caza, cena / sena (for s/z distinction in Latin American vs Peninsular)
- pollo / polo, calle / cale

**Assess and record:** Which pairs the learner distinguishes reliably. Note perception vs production
gaps (can hear the difference but can't produce it, or vice versa).

**Scaling:** Phase A: 4 pairs, isolated words only. Phase B: add context sentences. Phase C: embed
pairs in rapid-fire sentences, learner identifies which word was used.

## Activity 2: Vowel Purity Check

**When to use:** Phase A. Learner diphthongizes Spanish vowels (English habit), reduces unstressed
vowels to schwa, or produces tense/lax distinctions that don't exist in Spanish.

**How to run:**
1. Say 3-4 words with the target vowel. Exaggerate purity slightly.
2. Learner repeats each word. Listen for diphthong creep or schwa substitution.
3. Contrast with the English cognate if helpful: "mesa" vs English "may-suh."
4. Drill the hardest vowel in a short phrase: "Eso es especial."

**Example items:**
- /e/: mesa, pelo, enero, especial (watch for English "ay" diphthong)
- /o/: como, solo, otro, gobierno (watch for English "oh-w" diphthong)
- /a/ in unstressed position: banana, casa, manzana (watch for schwa)
- /u/: mucho, gusto, tú, seguro (watch for English lax "uh")

**Assess and record:** Which vowels are consistently pure vs which drift. Note whether the issue is
positional (unstressed syllables only) or general.

**Scaling:** Phase A: isolated words, 1 vowel per drill. Phase B: full sentences with mixed vowels.

## Activity 3: Stress Pattern Practice

**When to use:** Phase A+. Learner misplaces stress, especially on cognates (information vs
información) or doesn't distinguish stress-meaning pairs.

**How to run:**
1. Present 5-6 words. Learner marks the stressed syllable (clap, tap, or say it louder).
2. Mix patterns: agudas (last syllable), llanas (penultimate), esdrújulas (antepenultimate).
3. Present 2-3 stress-meaning pairs. Learner produces both with correct stress.
4. Read a short sentence aloud, learner identifies the stressed syllable of each content word.

**Example items:**
- Agudas: hablar, café, información, alemán
- Llanas: casa, hombre, importante, hermano
- Esdrújulas: pájaro, médico, teléfono, música
- Stress-meaning pairs: papá/papa, habló/hablo, práctico/practico

**Assess and record:** Whether the learner can identify stress patterns and whether production
matches. Note specific patterns that cause trouble (e.g., always stressing penultimate).

**Scaling:** Phase A: isolated words with obvious patterns. Phase B: stress-meaning pairs and
sentences. Phase C: identify stress in connected speech at natural speed.

## Activity 4: Connected Speech Shadowing

**When to use:** Phase B+. Learner produces words correctly in isolation but sounds choppy in
connected speech. Triggered by stilted rhythm during conversation practice.

**How to run:**
1. Say a short phrase (4-6 words) at natural speed. Don't slow down.
2. Learner repeats, trying to match rhythm and linking — not just the words.
3. If they struggle, break it into 2-word chunks, then rebuild to full phrase.
4. Repeat 3-4 phrases. Focus on linking (consonant-to-vowel) and elision.

**Example items:**
- "Vamos a ver" (linking: vamo-sa-ver)
- "Estoy en la oficina" (linking: estoy-en-la-oficina, natural elision)
- "No se lo he dicho" (weak pronoun chain, natural speed)
- "¿Qué te parece si vamos al cine?" (question rhythm + linking)

**Assess and record:** Whether the learner links words or inserts pauses between each. Note
specific linking patterns that are difficult.

**Scaling:** Phase B: 4-6 word phrases, slow-to-natural speed. Phase C: 8-10 word sentences at
natural speed. Phase D: short paragraph chunks with varied intonation.

## Activity 5: Sound-Spelling Correspondence

**When to use:** Phase B+. Learner misspells dictated words or mispronounces words based on
spelling. Triggered by consistent spelling errors in journal or homework.

**How to run:**
1. Dictate 6-8 words. Learner writes them down (or types in chat).
2. Review together. For each error, state the rule: "ge/gi always sounds like /x/."
3. Reverse: show 4 written words, learner pronounces them. Check for spelling-based errors.
4. Give 2-3 new words using the same patterns. Learner predicts spelling before seeing it.

**Example items:**
- c/s/z: cena, zapato, sociedad, cerveza, azúcar
- g/j: gente, joven, girar, jugar, generoso, jefe
- ll/y: calle, yerno, lluvia, yema, llegar
- h (silent): hombre, hacer, ahora, hay, huevo
- qu/cu: queso, cuando, quiero, cuidar

**Assess and record:** Which sound-spelling mappings are solid vs which cause confusion. Track
whether errors are production-based (mispronunciation) or perception-based (misspelling).

**Scaling:** Phase B: common words, one pattern per drill. Phase C: mixed patterns in a single
dictation, including less common words.

## Activity 6: Intonation Contour Practice

**When to use:** Phase C+. Learner uses flat or English-patterned intonation. Triggered by
questions that sound like statements or statements that sound uncertain.

**How to run:**
1. Demonstrate three contours with the same base sentence:
   - Statement (falling): "Tienes hambre."
   - Yes/no question (rising): "¿Tienes hambre?"
   - Information question (falling from wh-word): "¿Cuándo tienes hambre?"
2. Learner produces all three. Provide feedback on the contour shape.
3. Give 3-4 new base sentences. Learner produces each as statement, yes/no, and wh-question.
4. Short dialogue: tutor and learner alternate, each must use correct intonation for their intent.

**Example items:**
- Base: "Vamos al parque" -> statement / yes-no / "¿Cuándo vamos al parque?"
- Base: "Ella habla español" -> statement / yes-no / "¿Quién habla español?"
- Base: "El tren sale a las tres" -> statement / yes-no / "¿A qué hora sale el tren?"

**Assess and record:** Whether the learner differentiates the three contour types. Note if one type
is consistently problematic (usually yes/no questions for English speakers).

**Scaling:** Phase C: isolated sentences, exaggerated contours. Phase D: embedded in natural
dialogue, subtle contour differences, emotional intonation (surprise, disbelief, sarcasm).
