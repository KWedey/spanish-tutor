# Storytelling

## Purpose

Develops narrative production in past tenses. Requires sustained, coherent use of preterite and
imperfect together — which is harder than isolated drills. Used in Stage 4 (Communicative
Practice) of the B-04 (preterite vs imperfect) teaching sequence, and as a fluency-building
activity for Phase C+. Assesses `performance_unscaffolded` for preterite/imperfect integration.

## Phase Suitability

- **Phase B:** Retell a simple story from tutor prompts. Scaffolded, familiar vocabulary.
  Focus on consistent past tense use (don't drift back to present).
- **Phase C:** Create a story from a picture sequence or minimal prompt. Less scaffolding.
  Connectors (de repente, mientras tanto, al final) expected.
- **Phase D:** Improvisational or collaborative storytelling with no preparation. Complex
  characters, unexpected events. Natural pacing and style.

## Setup

Select a storytelling format matched to the learner's current phase:
- **Phase B:** Prepare 4-6 tutor prompts that walk through a simple story arc.
- **Phase C:** Find or describe a sequence of 4-5 pictures (can be described in text:
  "Picture 1 shows a woman at a train station with a large suitcase...").
- **Phase D:** Collaborative — each participant adds 2-3 sentences, alternating turns.

Do not pre-teach vocabulary before the activity. The learner should manage gaps using repair
strategies or circumlocution. That's part of what storytelling assesses.

## How to Run

### Basic Version (Phase B)

1. Set the scene with a brief prompt: "Tell me about a time you were late for something
   important. Use past tense throughout."
2. If the learner stalls, offer a content prompt (not a language prompt): "What happened
   first?" or "How did the other person react?"
3. Do not correct tense errors mid-story — note them silently.
4. After the story: 2-3 targeted corrections on tense usage (preterite vs imperfect). One
   thing done well.
5. Optional: ask the learner to retell the same story in 3 sentences. Compression forces
   them to identify what was essential.

### Advanced Version (Phase C-D)

**Picture-sequence storytelling:**
1. Describe the sequence of pictures (or provide a link to an actual image set).
2. Learner narrates the full story without interruption: 3-5 minutes.
3. Tutor takes notes on: tense distribution, connector use, vocabulary range, coherence.
4. Debrief: 2 corrections, 2 things done well, 1 suggestion for more natural phrasing.

**Collaborative storytelling (Phase D):**
1. Tutor opens: "Era una noche oscura y el pueblo estaba completamente vacío..."
2. Learner continues for 2-3 sentences.
3. Tutor continues, optionally introducing a complication or twist.
4. Alternate until the story reaches a natural end (10-15 turns total).
5. No corrections during the story. Brief debrief after: what worked, what to refine.

## What to Assess

Record after the activity:

```yaml
observations:
  - concept: "B-04-preterite-vs-imperfect"
    context: unscaffolded
    assessment: competent  # struggling or competent
storytelling_notes: "Good narrative arc. Consistent preterite for completed events. Used
  'de repente' and 'al final' as connectors. Imperfect for background was mostly correct —
  one slip (used preterite for habitual description). Vocabulary range is expanding."
```

Also note:
- Connector use: are they advancing the story with "y, y, y" or using varied connectors?
- Self-repair: when they lose the thread, do they recover in Spanish or switch to English?
- Narrative complexity: single-line events or multiple characters, motivations, outcomes?

## Variations

- **Retell-a-story:** Tutor tells a short story (5-6 sentences) in Spanish. Learner retells
  it from memory. Removes content burden so the learner can focus entirely on past tense form.
  Good Phase B introduction to the activity format.
- **Photo narration:** Show a single photo (describe it in text: "There's a photo of an elderly
  man sitting alone in a café at night"). Learner narrates what might have happened before or
  after the photo was taken. Encourages imagination alongside past tense use.
- **Story completion:** Tutor provides the opening (2-3 sentences) and the ending (1-2 sentences).
  Learner must fill in the middle. Constrained creativity reduces blank-page anxiety. Phase B-C.

## Example Implementation

**Phase C | Picture-sequence storytelling | 5-picture sequence description**

Picture sequence described:
1. A young woman at an airport, looking at a departures board with a worried expression.
2. A taxi driver outside, gesturing urgently at his watch.
3. The woman in the taxi, looking out the window at stopped traffic.
4. The plane on the runway, doors closing.
5. The woman sitting alone at an airport café, calling someone on her phone.

---

**Tutor:** Aquí tienes una secuencia de cinco imágenes. Nárrame la historia completa en
pasado. Tómate el tiempo que necesites.

**Learner:** Había una mujer en el aeropuerto. Miraba el panel de las salidas y estaba...
muy preocupada porque su vuelo salía muy pronto. Afuera, el taxista le decía que se apurara.
Tomaron un taxi, pero había mucho tráfico. La mujer miraba por la ventana y pensaba que iba
a perder el avión. Y en efecto, el avión... despegó sin ella. Las puertas se cerraron antes
de que llegara. Al final, se sentó sola en una cafetería del aeropuerto y llamó a alguien
— probablemente para explicar la situación.

---

**Tutor debrief:**

"Excelente — la historia es muy coherente y los tiempos están bien. Dos cosas:

1. 'El avión despegó sin ella' — perfecto. Pero antes dijiste 'la mujer miraba por la ventana
   y pensaba' — bien, eso es imperfecto para estado mental. Muy buena distinción.
2. 'Antes de que llegara' — ¡subjuntivo! Lo usaste perfectamente y de manera natural.
   Es un uso de C-02 (subjuntivo tras la conjunción adverbial temporal 'antes de que')
   que salió espontáneo. Eso es una señal muy positiva.

Una pequeña sugerencia: después de 'Y en efecto,' podrías decir 'para su mala suerte' —
suena más narrativo. Guárdalo como expresión."
