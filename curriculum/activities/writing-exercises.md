# Writing Exercises

## Purpose

Develops written production and reveals grammar gaps that stay hidden in conversation. Because the
learner has time to think and edit while writing, written work is held to a higher accuracy bar
than spoken output. Used as homework (journal, compositions) and reviewed during the Review block
of the following session.

## Phase Suitability

- **Phase B:** Journal entries (3-5 sentences). Guided prompts. Familiar vocabulary only. Errors
  are expected and treated as learning data, not failures.
- **Phase C:** Short compositions — emails, opinion paragraphs, summaries. Independent paragraphs
  without a step-by-step prompt.
- **Phase D:** Essays (multi-paragraph), creative writing, professional correspondence. Attention
  to register, cohesion, and style.

## Setup

When assigning writing homework, always:
1. Give a specific prompt — open-ended prompts produce shorter, lower-effort work.
2. State a target length: "3-5 sentences" or "1 paragraph (5-8 sentences)" or "200-250 words."
3. State the focus: "Use at least 3 different verb tenses" or "Practice the subjunctive triggers
   we covered today."

Writing is reviewed in the session after it was assigned. Do not review writing in the same
session it was assigned.

## How to Run

### Basic Version (Phase B)

1. Ask the learner to read their journal entry aloud. This catches errors they edited in writing
   but would have made in speech.
2. While they read: note errors silently. Identify the 2-3 most instructive ones.
3. After they finish: give specific praise for 1-2 things done well — not generic.
4. Correct 2-3 errors maximum:
   - Prioritize the current session focus concept.
   - Then high-frequency errors (ser/estar, agreement, pronouns).
   - Skip minor errors that are expected for this phase and not worth dwelling on.
5. Model the corrected form and ask the learner to repeat or rewrite it.

### Advanced Version (Phase C-D)

1. Ask for a brief oral summary of what they wrote. This verifies genuine engagement and reveals
   comprehension gaps.
2. Review the written piece holistically first: "The argument is clear" / "The structure is
   confusing in the second paragraph."
3. Select 2-3 structural or grammatical issues. Explain the pattern, not just the error.
4. Highlight register: is the word choice appropriate for the task? Formal compositions should
   avoid colloquialisms; informal emails should not sound academic.
5. For Phase D: discuss style and variety — are sentences all the same structure? Does the
   writing have rhythm?

## Correction Protocol

Higher written accuracy bar applies because the learner had time to think:
- An error made in writing that the learner knows how to correct verbally = **slip**. Note it,
  brief reminder, move on.
- An error made in writing that the learner cannot self-correct when shown = **gap**. Address
  with targeted drill or explanation.
- A pattern of written errors across 2+ journal entries = **persistent gap**. Log it in the
  skill map and increase focus.

Do NOT mark every error. A piece of writing covered in corrections is demoralizing and
counterproductive. Select the most instructive 2-3 and let the rest go for now.

## What to Assess

After reviewing writing in session, note in the session log:

```yaml
observations:
  - concept: "C-01-present-subjunctive"
    context: scaffolded
    attempts: 4
    errors: 1
writing_notes: "Good paragraph structure. Used 'sin embargo' as a connector unprompted — strong.
  Ser/estar confusion in sentence 3 (estaba for era). 'Interesante' overused — suggest synonyms."
```

Also note:
- Vocabulary range: are they using the same 10 words every time?
- Coherence: does the writing flow, or is it a list of disconnected sentences?
- Complexity: are sentence structures getting more varied over time?

## Variations

- **Guided journal:** Give a specific prompt and 2-3 sentence starters to reduce blank-page
  anxiety. Good for Phase B early sessions. Example: "Escribe sobre tu fin de semana. Empieza
  con: 'El viernes por la noche...' "
- **Summary writing:** After reading or listening homework, write a 3-5 sentence summary.
  Combines receptive and productive skills. Useful Phase B-C bridge.
- **Prompted rewrite:** Give a learner's own previous sentence with an error and ask them to
  correct it without hints. Tests whether feedback from prior sessions has transferred.

## Example Implementation

**Phase C | Assignment: Opinion paragraph (150 words) on a topic of the learner's choice**

**Learner's submitted paragraph:**

> *"Creo que la tecnología tiene muchos beneficios pero también tiene problemas. Por ejemplo,
> las redes sociales conecta a personas de todo el mundo. Pero también es posible que la gente
> se siente más sola que antes. Es importante que nosotros usamos la tecnología con cuidado.
> Sin embargo, pienso que el futuro va a ser mejor con inteligencia artificial."*

---

**Tutor review:**

Good things:
- Used "sin embargo" as a connector — excellent, and it's used correctly.
- "es posible que" trigger is almost correct — very close.
- Clear argument structure: benefit, problem, recommendation, conclusion.

Errors to address (select 2-3 most instructive):

1. "las redes sociales *conecta*" — agreement error. The subject is plural: "conectan."
   *(developmental — expected at this phase)*

2. "es posible que la gente se *siente* más sola" — subjunctive trigger not applied.
   Should be "se *sienta*." You identified the trigger correctly but used indicative.
   *(gap — reinforce C-02 subjunctive triggers)*

3. "es importante que nosotros *usamos*" — same pattern. After "es importante que," the
   subjunctive is required: "*usemos*." Drop the "nosotros" — it sounds unnatural here.
   *(same gap as above — both errors have the same root cause)*

**Post-review note:** Two subjunctive errors from the same trigger category. Log additional
`struggling` observation for C-02. Add a brief C-02 drill at the start of next session.
