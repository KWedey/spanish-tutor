# Session 9: Common Irregular Present Tense

## Concept
A-07 Common Irregular Present (ir, tener, querer, poder, hacer, decir, saber, conocer, hay)

## Prerequisites Check
- A-01 present regular conjugation — the learner must understand regular patterns to contrast with irregular ones
- A-06 gustar should be at least "introduced" — learner has encountered indirect object pronouns
- Previous concepts (A-01 through A-06) should be functional even if not yet "practicing"

## Session Plan

### Review (5-8 min)
**Gustar verification:** "What do you like about learning Spanish?" Tests gustar in an authentic context — not a drill, a real question. Listen for correct structure.

**Homework check:** Review the 8 gustar sentences from session 8. Check for correct pronoun use, singular/plural agreement, and article inclusion. Offer 1-2 corrections.

**Speechling check:** "How are the recordings going? Is the coach feedback helpful?" Verify continued use.

**Quick multi-concept warm-up:** "Describe your favorite meal using gustar and food vocabulary" — combines A-06 (gustar) + tier1-food vocabulary + A-03 (gender on food nouns).

### New Material (15-20 min)
Load `curriculum/grammar/A-foundation/07-present-irregular-common.md`.

**Framing (2 min):**
"You've learned the regular patterns — -ar, -er, -ir endings. Now we need the verbs that break those rules. The good news: these are the most useful verbs in Spanish. You'll use them every single conversation."

**Group 1 — Stem-changing verbs (5 min):**
- e→ie: querer (quiero, quieres, quiere, queremos, quieren), tener (tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tienen)
- o→ue: poder (puedo, puedes, puede, podemos, pueden)
- Pattern: the stem changes in all forms EXCEPT nosotros. "The boot shape — it changes everywhere except the 'we' form."
- Quick drill: "I want..." "You can..." "She has..." — learner conjugates.

**Group 2 — Yo-irregular verbs (4 min):**
- hacer → hago (but haces, hace, hacemos are predictable)
- saber → se (but sabes, sabe, sabemos are predictable)
- conocer → conozco (but conoces, conoce, conocemos are predictable)
- Pattern: only the yo form is truly irregular. The rest follow regular or stem-changing patterns.

**Saber vs conocer distinction (3 min):**
- Saber = know facts, know how to do something. "Se hablar espanol." "Se donde vives."
- Conocer = know/be familiar with people, places, things. "Conozco a tu hermano." "Conozco Madrid."
- "Saber is head knowledge. Conocer is experience knowledge. You 'saber' that Paris is in France. You 'conocer' Paris because you've been there."

**Group 3 — Fully irregular (3 min):**
- ir: voy, vas, va, vamos, van — completely irregular, must be memorized
- decir: digo, dices, dice, decimos, dicen — stem-change + yo-irregular
- hay: there is / there are — invariable, never changes form. "Hay un gato. Hay tres gatos."
- Drill: "Where do you go on weekends?" "What do you say when...?" "Is there a...?"

### Practice (5-10 min)
**Weekend conversation:** "What do you want to do this weekend? What can you do? Where do you go on Saturdays? What do you usually make for dinner?"

This naturally elicits querer, poder, ir, hacer. Scaffold with verb prompts if needed: "Can you...? Do you want to...? Do you know how to...?"

Listen for regularized irregulars ("haco" for "hago," "sabo" for "se") and saber/conocer confusion. Note errors silently. After: 2-3 corrections focused on the most frequent irregular errors.

### Checkout (3-5 min)
**Anki:** Add 8-10 new cards — irregular verb yo-forms (voy, tengo, quiero, puedo, hago, digo, se, conozco), hay, and saber vs conocer distinction.

**Homework (25-30 min total):**
- Anki review (10 min)
- Language Transfer: listen to 2 more episodes (15 min each — suggest at least 1 this week)
- Write 6 sentences using at least 4 different irregular verbs about what you want to do, can do, and know (10 min)
- Dreaming Spanish: 10-15 min — listen for irregular verbs in context (especially ir, tener, querer)

**Optional tool: Whisper transcription**
If the learner is tech-comfortable and interested: "There's a tool called Whisper that can transcribe your spoken Spanish. You speak, it writes what you said. It's a way to check your pronunciation on your own." Setup is optional — introduce the concept now, full setup only if the learner wants it.

## Vocabulary Focus
Continue `curriculum/vocabulary/tier1-survival/food-restaurant.md` — expand into ordering vocabulary: quiero/quisiera, me gustaria, para mi, la cuenta, mesero/camarero. These pair with irregular verbs (querer) and gustar-type constructions from session 8.

## Learner Observation Targets
- **Regularization errors:** The most common error type — learner applies regular patterns to irregulars ("haco," "teno," "sabo"). Track which verbs get regularized and how persistently.
- **Saber vs conocer confusion:** Does the learner default to one for everything? Which direction? This distinction has no English parallel.
- **Verb avoidance:** Does the learner avoid irregular verbs in free production, sticking to regular verbs they're confident with? Avoidance is informative — it means they know the form is uncertain.
- **Cognitive load accumulation:** This is session 9 of 10. The learner has 7 grammar concepts in play. Watch for signs of overload: longer pauses, more English fallback, less willingness to attempt complex sentences.

## Adjustment Notes
- If gustar is still very shaky: weave gustar practice into the irregular verb drills. "Do you like to travel? (Te gusta viajar?) Where do you go? (Adonde vas?)" This reinforces both simultaneously.
- If the learner memorizes irregular forms quickly: push into tener expressions (tener hambre, tener sueno, tener razon, tener anos) — these are extremely high-frequency and practical.
- If cognitive load is high: teach only Group 1 (stem-changing) this session. Defer yo-irregulars and fully irregulars to session 10 warm-up or post-onboarding sessions. Better to learn 3 verbs well than 9 verbs poorly.
- If the learner wants Whisper setup: help them install it, but cap the setup time at 5 minutes. It should not eat into grammar teaching time.
