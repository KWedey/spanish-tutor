# Session 4: Articles, Prepositions, and Personal 'a'

## Concept
A-04 Articles, Prepositions, and Personal 'a'

## Prerequisites Check
- A-03 gender knowledge — learner can assign el/la to common nouns
- A-01 present tense conjugation functional
- Repair phrases approaching automatic use

## Session Plan

### Review (5-8 min)
**Gender agreement warm-up:** Tutor says a noun, learner responds with el or la:
- libro (el), mesa (la), dia (el), mano (la), casa (la), coche (el), agua (el — but feminine), ciudad (la)

Correct and explain any errors. This warm-up also primes the article concepts.

**Homework verification:** Review the 5 description sentences from session 3. Check for gender/number agreement. Ask a follow-up: "You wrote your brother is tall — is he also funny?"

### New Material (15-20 min)
Load `curriculum/grammar/A-foundation/04-articles-prepositions.md`.

**Articles (5 min):**
- Definite: el, la, los, las — learner already knows gender, now add the article
- Indefinite: un, una, unos, unas
- When Spanish uses articles and English doesn't: "Me gusta **el** cafe" (not just "cafe")
- Contractions: a + el = al, de + el = del — "Voy **al** parque. Vengo **del** trabajo."

**Common prepositions (5 min):**
- a (to), de (from/of), en (in/on/at), con (with), sin (without), para (for — basic usage only), sobre (on/about), entre (between)
- Key English-Spanish mismatches: "pensar **en**" (think about), "sonar **con**" (dream about), "depender **de**" (depend on)

**L1 interference preemption:** "Prepositions are where English and Spanish really disagree. In English you 'think about' something. In Spanish you 'think in' something — 'pensar en.' You can't translate prepositions word-for-word."

**Personal 'a' (5 min):**
- Required before human direct objects: "Veo **a** mi madre" not "Veo mi madre"
- Not used for things: "Veo la mesa" (no 'a')
- Also used with pets: "Quiero **a** mi perro"

Stage 1 activity: Show pairs — "Veo la mesa. Veo **a** Maria." Ask: "What's different? Why do you think the 'a' appears in one but not the other?"

### Practice (5-10 min)
**Location and description practice:**
- "Where is your phone right now?" (en la mesa, en mi bolsillo)
- "Where do you work?" (en una oficina, en un restaurante)
- "Tell me about your neighborhood" — forces prepositions: cerca de, lejos de, al lado de, entre

Listen for missing personal 'a' and English preposition transfers. Note patterns silently; correct 2-3 at the end.

### Pronunciation Check-in (2-3 min)
Connected speech — in natural Spanish, articles link smoothly to the noun that follows. English speakers tend to insert tiny pauses between words; Spanish flows across word boundaries:
- "el agua" — the /l/ connects directly to the /a/, sounding like "e-LA-gua"
- "la escuela" — "laes-CUE-la," no gap between "la" and "escuela"
- "en una oficina" — the words chain together: "e-NU-nao-fi-CI-na"

Have the learner say "en la mesa" and "cerca de la escuela" at a comfortable pace, trying to link words without pausing between the article and noun. Speed is not the goal — smooth connections are.

### Checkout (3-5 min)
**Anki:** Add 8 new cards — contractions (al, del), personal 'a' examples, common preposition phrases (cerca de, lejos de, al lado de, encima de).

**No new tool setup this session** — consolidate Anki, SpanishDict, and Dreaming Spanish habits.

**Homework (20-25 min total):**
- Anki review (10 min)
- Write 5 sentences describing where things are located — must use prepositions and articles (10 min)
- Dreaming Spanish: 10-15 min superbeginner content (10-15 min)

## Vocabulary Focus
Continue `curriculum/vocabulary/tier1-survival/basic-descriptions.md` — add location words: cerca, lejos, arriba, abajo, al lado de, encima de, debajo de. These reinforce preposition practice naturally.

## Learner Observation Targets
- **Preposition transfer:** Which English preposition habits appear? Track the specific ones (e.g., always using "en" for "on" and "in" without distinction).
- **Personal 'a' adoption:** This has no English equivalent. Does the learner start using it naturally, or does it require constant reminding?
- **Contraction compliance:** Do they remember al and del, or do they produce "a el" and "de el"?
- **Cumulative load:** Session 4 is the last session before a high-severity concept (ser/estar). Monitor whether the learner is keeping up or feeling stretched.

## Adjustment Notes
- If gender agreement is still shaky: weave extra gender practice into the article drills (every article requires correct gender). This session naturally reinforces A-03.
- If the learner picks up articles and prepositions quickly: spend extra time on personal 'a' — it is the hardest part of this session and critical for B-06 (direct object pronouns).
- If the learner seems fatigued by accumulated concepts: keep the practice light. Favor a simple "describe your room" activity over a complex neighborhood description. Session 5 (ser/estar) is demanding — the learner should not arrive exhausted.
