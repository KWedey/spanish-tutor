# Session 8: Gustar-Type Verbs

## Concept
A-06 Gustar-Type Verbs — HIGH-severity L1 interference

## Prerequisites Check
- A-01 present tense conjugation — needed to understand the reversed structure
- A-05 questions — learner can form questions (useful for "Te gusta...?" practice)
- Session 6 consolidation showed no major regressions in A-01 through A-04

Also load `curriculum/tutor-guides/l1-interference-protocol.md` — gustar construction is HIGH severity, HIGH fossilization risk. This is the most conceptually difficult structure in Phase A.

## Session Plan

### Review (5-8 min)
**Questions warm-up:** Learner asks 5 questions using different question words — verifies session 7 material is retained. Listen for double-negative avoidance and correct gently if it appears.

**Speechling check:** "Did you record your sentences? How did the coach feedback go? Any surprises?" Verify they actually used the tool — ask which sentence they recorded.

**Quick negation check:** "Do you have a cat?" Expecting: "No, no tengo ningún gato" (or similar). Tests double-negative from session 7.

### New Material (15-20 min)
Load `curriculum/grammar/A-foundation/06-gustar-type-verbs.md`.

**Explicit L1 preemption (3 min):**
"This structure is completely backwards from English. Your brain will resist it. That's normal and expected."

"In English: 'I like coffee' — you are the subject, you do the liking. In Spanish: 'Me gusta el café' — the coffee is the subject, it pleases you. You are the object. This is not a minor difference. It's a completely reversed way of thinking about the sentence."

**Stage 1 — Noticing (5 min):**
Show two sentences: "Me gusta el café. Me gustan los libros."
Ask: "Why does the verb change? It's not because of 'me.'" Guide the learner to see that the verb agrees with el café (singular → gusta) and los libros (plural → gustan).

Build the structure step by step:
- Indirect object pronoun (me, te, le, nos, les) + gusta/gustan + the thing liked
- The thing liked is the SUBJECT — the verb agrees with IT, not with the person

**Stage 2 — Pronoun practice (4 min):**
- Me gusta (I like), Te gusta (you like), Le gusta (he/she likes), Nos gusta (we like), Les gusta (they like)
- Emphasis/clarification: "A mí me gusta..." / "A ti te gusta..." / "A él le gusta..."
- Drill: Tutor gives a person and a thing, learner builds the sentence. "Your sister / dogs" → "A ella le gustan los perros."

**Stage 3 — Expand to other gustar-type verbs (3 min):**
- encantar (to love/enchant), molestar (to bother), importar (to matter), interesar (to interest), doler (to hurt)
- "Me encanta la música." "Me molesta el ruido." "Me duele la cabeza."
- Same reversed structure — the thing is always the subject.

### Practice (5-10 min)
**Likes/dislikes conversation:** "Tell me 5 things you like and 3 things that bother you."

Scaffold if needed: provide noun prompts ("What about food? Music? Sports? Your job?"). Listen carefully for the most common error: "Yo gusto el café" (treating gustar like English "like"). Do NOT interrupt to correct. Note all instances.

After: Give 2-3 corrections on the gustar structure specifically. Highlight correct uses: "You said 'Me gustan los perros' — that plural agreement was exactly right."

**Extension if time allows:** "Ask me what I like" — forces question formation with gustar: "Te gusta...?" This combines A-05 (questions) with A-06 (gustar).

### Checkout (3-5 min)
**Anki:** Add 8 new cards — gustar constructions: me gusta/gustan, te gusta/gustan, le gusta/gustan, plus encantar and molestar examples. Include the "A mí me gusta" emphasis form.

**Homework (25-30 min total):**
- Anki review (10 min)
- Write 8 sentences about your likes and dislikes — must use at least 3 different gustar-type verbs and both singular and plural forms (10 min)
- Speechling: record 3 gustar sentences — focus on natural rhythm of "Me gusta..." (5-10 min)
- Dreaming Spanish: 10-15 min superbeginner content — listen for gustar in context

## Vocabulary Focus
Begin `curriculum/vocabulary/tier1-survival/food-restaurant.md` — introduce common food words: pollo, carne, pescado, arroz, ensalada, café, agua, cerveza. These pair naturally with gustar: "Me gusta el pollo. No me gusta el pescado. Me encanta el café."

## Learner Observation Targets
- **Reversed structure adoption:** This is the key metric. Does the learner produce "Me gusta X" or revert to "Yo gusto X"? Track error frequency — this is a high-fossilization-risk pattern.
- **Number agreement:** Does the learner correctly use gustan (plural) when the subject is plural? "Me gustan los libros" not "Me gusta los libros."
- **Emotional response to the reversal:** Some learners find this fascinating, others find it frustrating. Calibrate encouragement accordingly.
- **Integration with prior concepts:** Can the learner combine gustar with correct articles ("Me gusta **el** café" not "Me gusta café") and adjectives ("Me gustan los perros **grandes**")?

## Adjustment Notes
- If the learner cannot produce the gustar structure after 15 minutes: simplify. Focus only on "me gusta" and "me gustan" — defer other pronouns (te, le, nos, les) to session 9 warm-up. The singular/plural agreement is the priority.
- If prior concepts from sessions 2-5 are regressing under the cognitive load of gustar: note this for the session log. Gustar is demanding — some temporary regression in other areas is normal and expected.
- If the learner grasps the structure quickly: push into the other gustar-type verbs (encantar, molestar, doler) and practice the emphasis form ("A mí me gusta, pero a ti te gusta..."). Assign more complex homework sentences.
- If Speechling recordings from session 7 showed pronunciation issues: incorporate 2 minutes of targeted pronunciation work during the warm-up. Don't let pronunciation problems compound silently.
