# Session 5: Ser vs Estar

## Concept
A-02 Ser vs Estar — HIGH-severity L1 interference

## Prerequisites Check
- A-01 present tense conjugation (needed to conjugate both ser and estar)
- A-03 gender agreement (needed for descriptions that use ser/estar)
- A-04 articles (needed to build noun phrases in practice sentences)
- Repair phrases should be automatic by this session

Also load `curriculum/tutor-guides/l1-interference-protocol.md` — this is the first high-severity L1 concept.

## Session Plan

### Review (5-8 min)
**Quick warm-up covering sessions 2-4:** Ask a question that requires articles + adjective agreement + present tense:
- "Describe your best friend" — tests A-01 (verbs), A-03 (agreement), A-04 (articles)
- "What do you do on a typical Saturday?" — tests A-01 (present tense)

**Homework check:** Review session 4 location sentences. Verify preposition and personal 'a' usage.

**Repair phrase check:** At some point in the warm-up, say something slightly too fast in Spanish. Does the learner use a repair phrase automatically? If not, prompt: "Remember your tools!"

### New Material (15-20 min)
Load `curriculum/grammar/A-foundation/02-ser-vs-estar.md`.

**Explicit L1 preemption (2 min):**
"In English, 'to be' is one verb. In Spanish, there are TWO: ser and estar. They are NOT interchangeable. Using the wrong one can change meaning entirely. 'Es aburrido' means 'he's a boring person.' 'Esta aburrido' means 'he's bored right now.' This will be confusing — that's completely normal. It takes time."

**Stage 1 — Sorting (5 min):**
Show 12 sentences — 6 with ser, 6 with estar. Learner sorts them into two groups. Then ask: "What do the ser sentences have in common? What about the estar sentences?"

**Categorization (5 min):**
- **Ser:** identity (soy profesor), origin (soy de Mexico), characteristics (es alto), time (son las tres), material (es de madera), possession (es mio)
- **Estar:** location (estoy en casa), temporary states (estoy cansado), emotions (esta contento), results of actions (esta roto), progressive (esta hablando — preview only)

Use a mnemonic if it helps the learner's style. Some find DOCTOR/PLACE useful (ser: Description, Occupation, Characteristic, Time, Origin, Relation / estar: Position, Location, Action, Condition, Emotion).

**Contrast pairs drill (3 min):**
- "Es aburrido" (boring person) vs "Esta aburrido" (bored)
- "Es listo" (clever) vs "Esta listo" (ready)
- "Es malo" (bad person) vs "Esta malo" (sick/tastes bad)
- "Es rico" (wealthy) vs "Esta rico" (delicious)

### Practice (5-10 min)
**Mixed identity and state conversation:**
"Tell me about yourself — who you are, where you're from, what you do — AND how you're feeling right now."

This forces mixing ser (identity: soy Kyle, soy de..., soy...) with estar (state: estoy cansado, estoy contento, estoy en...). Listen carefully for confusion between the two. Do NOT interrupt to correct. Note errors for review.

After the conversation: give 2-3 corrections on ser/estar specifically. Highlight any correct uses: "You said 'estoy cansado' — that's exactly right. Tired is a temporary state, so it's estar."

### Checkout (3-5 min)
**Anki:** Add 8 new cards — ser/estar contrast pairs (same adjective, different meaning). Include both conjugated forms: soy/estoy, es/esta, son/estan.

**Tool setup: Language Transfer and graded reader**
- Language Transfer: "Complete Spanish" episodes 1-3 on YouTube or podcast app. Free, ~15 min each. "This is a great complement because it teaches through thinking, not memorizing."
- Graded reader: Recommend an A1 graded reader. "Read the first chapter this week. Look up words you don't know on SpanishDict. Don't try to understand every word — get the story."

**Homework (25-30 min total):**
- Anki review (10 min)
- Language Transfer episodes 1-3 (15 min per episode, suggest at least 1 this week)
- Write 6 sentences: 3 using ser and 3 using estar, about yourself (10 min)
- Start graded reader — first 2-3 pages (10 min)

## Vocabulary Focus
Begin `curriculum/vocabulary/tier1-survival/numbers-time-dates.md` — introduce time expressions that use ser: "Son las tres," "Es la una," "Son las diez de la manana." Time is a natural entry point because it reinforces ser in a concrete, high-frequency context.

## Learner Observation Targets
- **Ser/estar confusion severity:** This is the first data point for the highest-risk L1 pattern. How often does the learner default to one verb for everything? Which direction (all ser? all estar?)?
- **Contrast pair comprehension:** Do they understand that the same adjective changes meaning? Or do they treat ser/estar as random?
- **Self-monitoring:** When they make a ser/estar error and you recast, do they notice the correction?
- **Cognitive load:** This concept arrives on top of 3 previous sessions. Is the learner handling the accumulation, or showing signs of fatigue?

## Adjustment Notes
- If the learner is overwhelmed by ser/estar: focus only on the clearest cases — identity (ser) and location (estar). Defer the nuanced contrast pairs (aburrido, listo, rico) to future sessions. The basic distinction is more important than the edge cases.
- If previous concepts are shaky: spend more time in review. Ser/estar can wait one more session — consolidating present tense, gender, and articles is more valuable than rushing into a high-severity concept while foundations are unstable.
- If the learner grasps the distinction quickly: push into the contrast pairs and introduce the mnemonic. Assign extra ser/estar practice sentences as homework.
- If repair phrases are NOT automatic by this session: flag this in session notes. They must be solid before onboarding ends. Add repair phrase micro-drills to future sessions.
