# Indirect Speech — Reporting What Others Said

## Overview
Indirect speech lets the learner report what someone else said, asked, or requested without quoting them directly: "She said she was coming" instead of "She said, 'I am coming.'" In Spanish, this requires systematic tense backshift and — critically — subjunctive when reporting commands, requests, or wishes. This concept weaves together tense knowledge, subjunctive usage, and narrative skill into one integrated package.

## When to Teach
- Phase: C
- Prerequisites: C-01-present-subjunctive, C-02-subjunctive-triggers, B-04-preterite-vs-imperfect
- L1 interference to preempt: indirect-speech-subjunctive (MEDIUM severity)

## The Pattern

### The Basic Structure

Direct speech quotes the exact words:
- María dijo: "**Estoy** cansada." (María said: "I am tired.")

Indirect speech reports the content:
- María dijo que **estaba** cansada. (María said that she was tired.)

The reporting verb (dijo, contó, explicó) is followed by **que** and the reported content.

### Tense Backshift Rules

When the reporting verb is in the past (dijo, contó, preguntó), the verb in the reported clause shifts back one step:

| Direct Speech (Original) | Indirect Speech (Reported) | Example |
|--------------------------|---------------------------|---------|
| **Present** (estoy) | → **Imperfect** (estaba) | "Estoy cansada." → Dijo que **estaba** cansada. |
| **Preterite** (comí) | → **Pluperfect** (había comido) | "Comí mucho." → Dijo que **había comido** mucho. |
| **Future** (vendré) | → **Conditional** (vendría) | "Vendré mañana." → Dijo que **vendría** al día siguiente. |
| **Present perfect** (he visto) | → **Pluperfect** (había visto) | "He visto esa película." → Dijo que **había visto** esa película. |
| **Imperfect** (comía) | → **Imperfect** (comía) — no change | "Comía mucho." → Dijo que **comía** mucho. |
| **Conditional** (iría) | → **Conditional** (iría) — no change | "Iría contigo." → Dijo que **iría** conmigo. |

**Key simplification:** The imperfect and conditional do not shift further. They are already "back" enough.

**When the reporting verb is in the present** (dice, cuenta), no shift is needed:
- María dice que **está** cansada. (María says she is tired.) — no change

### Subjunctive in Reported Commands and Requests

When reporting what someone **asked, requested, recommended, or demanded**, the reported clause uses **subjunctive**:

| Reporting Verb | Direct | Indirect |
|----------------|--------|----------|
| **pedir que** (ask/request) | "Ven aquí." | Me **pidió que viniera**. (He asked me to come.) |
| **recomendar que** (recommend) | "Estudia más." | Me **recomendó que estudiara** más. (She recommended I study more.) |
| **sugerir que** (suggest) | "Prueba este plato." | Me **sugirió que probara** ese plato. (She suggested I try that dish.) |
| **insistir en que** (insist) | "Quédate." | **Insistió en que me quedara**. (He insisted that I stay.) |
| **prohibir que** (forbid) | "No salgas." | Me **prohibió que saliera**. (He forbade me from going out.) |
| **rogar que** (beg) | "Ayúdame." | Me **rogó que lo ayudara**. (He begged me to help him.) |

**The rule:** If the original speech was a command, request, wish, or recommendation, the reported version uses subjunctive. This is because these verbs are subjunctive triggers (C-02) — "pidió que" works the same as "quería que."

### Reporting Questions

When reporting a question, the structure depends on the question type:

**Yes/no questions** use **si** (if/whether):
- Direct: "¿Vienes a la fiesta?" (Are you coming to the party?)
- Indirect: Me preguntó **si iba** a la fiesta. (He asked me if I was going to the party.)

**Information questions** keep the question word with its accent mark:
- Direct: "¿Dónde vives?" (Where do you live?)
- Indirect: Me preguntó **dónde vivía**. (He asked me where I lived.)

- Direct: "¿Cuándo llegas?" (When do you arrive?)
- Indirect: Me preguntó **cuándo llegaba**. (He asked me when I was arriving.)

- Direct: "¿Por qué estudias español?" (Why do you study Spanish?)
- Indirect: Me preguntó **por qué estudiaba** español. (He asked me why I studied Spanish.)

**The accent marks stay.** In reported questions, dónde, cuándo, cómo, qué, por qué, cuánto all retain their written accents. These accents distinguish question words from conjunctions (donde vs dónde, que vs qué).

### Common Reporting Verbs

**Statements:**
| Verb | Translation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **decir que** | to say that | Dijo que estaba cansada. |
| **contar que** | to tell/recount that | Me contó que había viajado a Perú. |
| **explicar que** | to explain that | Explicó que no podía venir. |
| **comentar que** | to comment/mention that | Comentó que la película era buena. |
| **afirmar que** | to affirm/state that | Afirmó que era inocente. |
| **admitir que** | to admit that | Admitió que se había equivocado. |

**Questions:**
| Verb | Translation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **preguntar si** | to ask if/whether | Preguntó si podía ir. |
| **preguntar + question word** | to ask where/when/etc. | Preguntó dónde vivía. |
| **querer saber si** | to want to know if | Quería saber si me gustaba. |

**Commands/Requests (+ subjunctive):**
| Verb | Translation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **pedir que** | to ask/request that | Me pidió que lo ayudara. |
| **recomendar que** | to recommend that | Me recomendó que estudiara más. |
| **sugerir que** | to suggest that | Sugirió que fuéramos al cine. |
| **ordenar que** | to order that | Ordenó que salieran. |
| **aconsejar que** | to advise that | Me aconsejó que descansara. |

### Other Shifts in Indirect Speech

Beyond the verb tense, other elements shift to reflect the reporter's perspective:

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| hoy (today) | ese día / aquel día (that day) |
| mañana (tomorrow) | al día siguiente (the next day) |
| ayer (yesterday) | el día anterior (the day before) |
| aquí (here) | allí (there) |
| este/esta (this) | ese/esa (that) |
| yo (I) | él/ella (he/she) |

## Examples in Context

1. Juan dijo que **estaba** enfermo. (Juan said he was sick.) — present → imperfect
2. Me contó que **había visitado** París el año pasado. (She told me she had visited Paris last year.) — preterite → pluperfect
3. Dijo que **vendría** al día siguiente. (He said he would come the next day.) — future → conditional
4. Me preguntó **si** quería ir al cine. (He asked me if I wanted to go to the movies.) — yes/no question
5. Me preguntó **dónde** trabajaba. (She asked me where I worked.) — information question
6. Mi madre me **pidió que limpiara** mi cuarto. (My mother asked me to clean my room.) — subjunctive
7. El doctor me **recomendó que descansara** más. (The doctor recommended that I rest more.) — subjunctive
8. Explicó que no **había podido** terminar el proyecto. (He explained that he hadn't been able to finish the project.) — preterite → pluperfect
9. Me **sugirió que probara** la comida local. (She suggested that I try the local food.) — subjunctive
10. María dice que **está** lista. (María says she is ready.) — present reporting verb, no shift

## L1 Interference

### indirect-speech-subjunctive (from l1-interference.yaml)

**English cause:** English rarely changes mood in reported speech. "He told me to come" uses an infinitive, not a subjunctive form. Spanish requires subjunctive in the same context: "Me dijo que viniera."

**Common L1-driven mistakes:**

| English Instinct | Wrong | Correct | Why |
|-----------------|-------|---------|-----|
| Use indicative in reported request | Me dijo que viene mañana. | Me dijo que **viniera** al día siguiente. | Reported requests require subjunctive |
| Use infinitive for reported command | Me pidió venir. | Me pidió que **viniera**. | Spanish uses que + subjunctive, not infinitive |
| Keep present tense in reported speech | Dijo que está cansada. | Dijo que **estaba** cansada. | Past reporting verb triggers backshift |
| Drop accent on question words | Me preguntó donde vivía. | Me preguntó **dónde** vivía. | Accents retained in indirect questions |

**Preemption script:** "When you report what someone said in English, the tense shifts naturally — 'She says she IS tired' becomes 'She said she WAS tired.' Spanish does the same thing. The tricky part is when you're reporting a command or request. In English you use an infinitive: 'He asked me to come.' In Spanish, you use que + subjunctive: 'Me pidió que viniera.' Think of it this way: pidió que is a subjunctive trigger, just like quería que."

**Persistence:** Medium — tied to broader subjunctive acquisition. As the learner internalizes subjunctive triggers from C-02, the indirect speech pattern follows.

## Common Errors

1. **No tense backshift.** "Dijo que está cansada" instead of "Dijo que estaba cansada." The learner keeps the original tense because English sometimes allows this in informal speech ("He said he is tired"). — L1 interference. Drill transformation: direct → indirect with past reporting verb.
2. **Indicative instead of subjunctive for reported commands.** "Me pidió que venía" instead of "Me pidió que viniera." The learner does not recognize the reported command as a subjunctive trigger. — L1 interference (indirect-speech-subjunctive). Explicitly connect pedir que to the WEIRDO triggers from C-02.
3. **Using infinitive for reported commands.** "Me pidió venir" instead of "Me pidió que viniera." English uses infinitives for this ("asked me to come"), so the learner maps directly. — L1 interference. Note: "Me pidió venir" can be grammatical if the subjects are the same, but with different subjects, que + subjunctive is required.
4. **Dropping accents on indirect question words.** "Me preguntó donde vivía" instead of "dónde vivía." The accent distinguishes the interrogative (dónde = where?) from the relative (donde = where). — Developmental. Reinforce: if the original sentence had a question mark, the question word keeps its accent.
5. **Incorrect time/place shifts.** "Dijo que vendría mañana" instead of "al día siguiente." The learner forgets that the perspective shifts from the original speaker to the reporter. — Developmental. Practice the full shift table (hoy → ese día, aquí → allí, etc.).

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Present five direct quotes with their indirect speech equivalents. Ask the learner to identify what changed in each pair:

- "Estoy cansado." → Dijo que estaba cansado.
- "Comí demasiado." → Dijo que había comido demasiado.
- "Vendré mañana." → Dijo que vendría al día siguiente.
- "¿Dónde vives?" → Me preguntó dónde vivía.
- "Estudia más." → Me pidió que estudiara más.

Guide toward noticing: the verb tense shifts, time words shift, and commands become subjunctive.

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
**Tense shift drill:** Give a direct quote and a reporting verb. The learner produces the indirect version:

1. Pedro: "Tengo hambre." (decir) → Pedro dijo que tenía hambre.
2. Ana: "Fui al médico." (contar) → Ana contó que había ido al médico.
3. Mi jefe: "Terminaré el informe." (afirmar) → Mi jefe afirmó que terminaría el informe.
4. Mi amiga: "¿Hablas francés?" (preguntar) → Mi amiga me preguntó si hablaba francés.
5. Mi mamá: "Limpia tu cuarto." (pedir) → Mi mamá me pidió que limpiara mi cuarto.

**Subjunctive recognition drill:** Classify these as statement (indicative) or command/request (subjunctive):
- "Dijo que venía." (statement — indicative)
- "Pidió que viniera." (request — subjunctive)
- "Contó que había salido." (statement — indicative)
- "Recomendó que descansara." (recommendation — subjunctive)

### Stage 3: Guided Production
Prompt: "I'm going to tell you some things, and I want you to report them back to me as if you're telling a friend what I said. I'll mix statements, questions, and requests."

Tutor says: "Estoy aprendiendo italiano." → Learner: "Me dijo que estaba aprendiendo italiano."
Tutor asks: "¿Te gusta la música?" → Learner: "Me preguntó si me gustaba la música."
Tutor requests: "Tráeme un café, por favor." → Learner: "Me pidió que le trajera un café."

Scaffold by providing the reporting verb at first, then let the learner choose their own.

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Prompt: "Think about a conversation you had recently — with a friend, family member, or coworker. Tell me what happened. What did they say? What did they ask you? Did they ask you to do anything? Report the whole conversation."

This naturally mixes all three indirect speech types: statements (dijo que...), questions (preguntó si/dónde/cuándo...), and requests (pidió que + subjunctive). The focus is on telling a real story, with indirect speech as the vehicle. Follow up with: "Now tell me about a piece of advice someone gave you. What did they recommend?"

## Dialect Notes
Indirect speech follows the same structural rules across all Spanish dialects. The tense backshift system and subjunctive requirement for reported commands are universal.

Minor regional notes:
- In **informal Mexican Spanish**, speakers sometimes skip the backshift when the reported information is still relevant: "Me dijo que viene mañana" (instead of "vendría") when the person is indeed coming tomorrow. This is common in speech but considered less precise in writing.
- The choice of reporting verb varies by region. **Mexican Spanish** commonly uses "me dijo que" and "me contó que." "Comentar que" is more formal. "Afirmar que" is formal/journalistic.
- In some dialects, "dizque" (allegedly, supposedly) is used as an informal reporting marker: "Dizque viene mañana." This is colloquial and region-specific (Mexico, Colombia, Andean countries).

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Correctly applies tense backshift in transformation drills. Chooses subjunctive for reported commands and requests. Uses si for yes/no indirect questions and retains accent marks on question words.
- **Unscaffolded:** Naturally reports conversations using dijo que, preguntó si/dónde, and pidió que + subjunctive. Shifts time references (hoy → ese día) without prompting. Self-corrects when backshift is missed.
- **Integrated:** Maintains correct indirect speech while narrating complex stories that mix direct dialogue, reported speech, and narrative. Switches between reporting statements, questions, and commands fluidly. Uses a variety of reporting verbs (not just "dijo").

## Connection Points
Indirect speech is a synthesis concept that requires strong foundations in several areas:

- **B-04** (preterite vs imperfect) is prerequisite because the tense backshift system requires confident use of past tenses.
- **C-01** (present subjunctive) and **C-02** (subjunctive triggers) provide the foundation for subjunctive in reported commands. Pedir que, recomendar que, and sugerir que are subjunctive triggers the learner has already practiced — now they appear in a new context.
- **C-06** (compound tenses) provides the pluperfect needed for preterite → pluperfect backshift.
- **D-01** (past subjunctive) deepens the subjunctive forms used in indirect speech. The imperfect subjunctive (viniera, estudiara) appears here in preview form.
- **D-06** (nuanced connectors) enables more sophisticated reporting: "Aunque dijo que vendría, sin embargo no apareció."

Common regression trigger: the learner may temporarily lose confidence in tense selection when simultaneously managing backshift, subjunctive choice, and narrative tenses (preterite vs imperfect). If this happens, simplify by drilling one indirect speech type at a time before mixing.
