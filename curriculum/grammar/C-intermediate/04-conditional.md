# Conditional — Hypotheticals and Politeness

## Overview
The conditional mood expresses what would happen, what someone would do, or what would be better. It is the backbone of polite requests ("¿Podrías ayudarme?"), hypothetical reasoning ("Sería mejor"), advice ("Yo que tú, estudiaría más"), and reported future in past ("Dijo que vendría"). Because it shares all 12 irregular stems with the formal future (C-03), it is taught immediately after -- the learner only needs to master new endings.

## When to Teach
- Phase: C
- Prerequisites: C-03-formal-future (same 12 irregular stems)
- L1 interference to preempt: none (English "would" maps well to the conditional)

## The Pattern

### Formation: Infinitive + Endings

Like the formal future, the conditional attaches endings to the **entire infinitive**. The endings are the same for all verb groups (-ar, -er, -ir):

| Person | Ending |
|--------|--------|
| yo | **-ía** |
| tú | **-ías** |
| él/ella/usted | **-ía** |
| nosotros | **-íamos** |
| vosotros | **-íais** |
| ellos/ustedes | **-ían** |

Note: all forms carry an accent mark on the **í**.

### Regular Conjugation

| Person | hablar | comer | vivir |
|--------|--------|-------|-------|
| yo | hablar**ía** | comer**ía** | vivir**ía** |
| tú | hablar**ías** | comer**ías** | vivir**ías** |
| él/ella/usted | hablar**ía** | comer**ía** | vivir**ía** |
| nosotros | hablar**íamos** | comer**íamos** | vivir**íamos** |
| vosotros | hablar**íais** | comer**íais** | vivir**íais** |
| ellos/ustedes | hablar**ían** | comer**ían** | vivir**ían** |

### The Same 12 Irregular Stems

The conditional uses the **exact same irregular stems** as the formal future. The only change is the ending (-ía instead of -é/-ás/-á):

**Group 1: Drop the -e- from the infinitive**

| Infinitive | Stem | Future (yo) | Conditional (yo) |
|-----------|------|-------------|-------------------|
| poder | podr- | podré | **podría** |
| querer | querr- | querré | **querría** |
| saber | sabr- | sabré | **sabría** |
| haber | habr- | habré | **habría** |
| caber | cabr- | cabré | **cabría** |

**Group 2: Replace the vowel with -d-**

| Infinitive | Stem | Future (yo) | Conditional (yo) |
|-----------|------|-------------|-------------------|
| tener | tendr- | tendré | **tendría** |
| poner | pondr- | pondré | **pondría** |
| salir | saldr- | saldré | **saldría** |
| venir | vendr- | vendré | **vendría** |
| valer | valdr- | valdré | **valdría** |

**Group 3: Shortened stems**

| Infinitive | Stem | Future (yo) | Conditional (yo) |
|-----------|------|-------------|-------------------|
| decir | dir- | diré | **diría** |
| hacer | har- | haré | **haría** |

### Four Core Uses

**1. Hypothetical situations**
Expressing what would happen or what would be the case:
- **Sería** mejor ir mañana. (It would be better to go tomorrow.)
- Yo no **haría** eso. (I wouldn't do that.)
- ¿Qué **harías** en mi lugar? (What would you do in my place?)

**2. Polite requests and softened statements**
The conditional makes requests more courteous:
- ¿**Podrías** ayudarme? (Could you help me?)
- ¿**Tendría** usted un momento? (Would you have a moment?)
- Me **gustaría** un café, por favor. (I'd like a coffee, please.)

**3. Advice**
Especially with "yo que tú" (if I were you):
- Yo que tú, **estudiaría** más. (If I were you, I'd study more.)
- Yo no **diría** nada. (I wouldn't say anything.)
- **Deberías** descansar. (You should rest.)

**4. Reported future in the past**
When someone said something would happen:
- Dijo que **vendría**. (He said he would come.)
- Prometió que **llamaría**. (She promised she would call.)
- Pensaba que **sería** más fácil. (I thought it would be easier.)

## Examples in Context

1. ¿**Podrías** pasarme la sal? (Could you pass me the salt?) -- polite request
2. Me **gustaría** visitar España algún día. (I'd like to visit Spain someday.) -- hypothetical
3. Yo que tú, **hablaría** con ella. (If I were you, I'd talk to her.) -- advice
4. Dijo que **vendría** a las ocho. (He said he'd come at eight.) -- reported future
5. **Sería** mejor salir temprano. (It would be better to leave early.) -- hypothetical
6. ¿Qué **harías** con un millón de dólares? (What would you do with a million dollars?) -- hypothetical
7. No **sabría** qué decir en esa situación. (I wouldn't know what to say in that situation.) -- hypothetical
8. Me prometió que **pondría** la mesa. (He promised me he'd set the table.) -- reported future
9. **Deberías** probar la comida mexicana. (You should try the Mexican food.) -- advice
10. Yo nunca **viviría** en una ciudad tan grande. (I would never live in such a big city.) -- hypothetical

## L1 Interference

While the English conditional ("would") maps well to the Spanish conditional, there is no high-severity L1 interference pattern. However, one important confusion deserves attention:

**The -ía trap: conditional vs imperfect**

Both the conditional and the imperfect indicative of -er/-ir verbs end in **-ía**:

| Form | Example | Meaning |
|------|---------|---------|
| Imperfect | com**ía** | I used to eat / I was eating |
| Conditional | comer**ía** | I would eat |

The difference is in the stem: the imperfect attaches -ía to the **bare stem** (com-), while the conditional attaches -ía to the **full infinitive** (comer-). This produces audibly different forms:

| Imperfect | Conditional | Distinction |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| com**ía** | comer**ía** | Extra syllable in conditional |
| viv**ía** | vivir**ía** | Extra syllable in conditional |
| ten**ía** | tendr**ía** | Different stem entirely |
| pon**ía** | pondr**ía** | Different stem entirely |
| ven**ía** | vendr**ía** | Different stem entirely |

For -ar verbs, there is no confusion: imperfect is -aba (hablaba) and conditional is -aría (hablaría) -- completely different.

**Preemption script:** "You already know the imperfect endings -ía for -er/-ir verbs. The conditional also uses -ía, but it attaches to the full infinitive, not the bare stem. Listen for the extra syllable: 'comía' is imperfect, 'comería' is conditional. For the irregulars, it's even clearer: 'tenía' vs 'tendría' -- completely different stems."

## Common Errors

1. **Confusing conditional -ía with imperfect -ía.** "Comía más verduras" (I used to eat more vegetables) when "Comería más verduras" (I would eat more vegetables) was intended. The learner hears -ía and defaults to the more familiar imperfect form. -- developmental. Drill contrastive pairs: comía/comería, vivía/viviría, tenía/tendría.
2. **Wrong irregular stems.** "Ponería" instead of "pondría," "tenería" instead of "tendría." The same stem errors as the future, carried over. -- developmental. Review the three groups from C-03 and practice applying conditional endings to each.
3. **Using conditional where subjunctive is needed.** "Quiero que vendrías" instead of "Quiero que vengas." The conditional expresses "would," but after subjunctive triggers, the subjunctive mood is required, not the conditional. -- overgeneralization. Reinforce: the conditional is a mood for hypotheticals, not a substitute for subjunctive after trigger expressions.
4. **Omitting the conditional for politeness.** "¿Puedes ayudarme?" when a more polite "¿Podrías ayudarme?" is socially appropriate. The conditional softens requests and is expected in formal or professional contexts. -- register awareness. Practice formal/informal request pairs.

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Present the 12 irregular stems from C-03 alongside their conditional forms. The learner already knows these stems. Ask: "You know that tendré means 'I will have.' What do you think tendría means?"

Pairs to present:
- hablaré → hablaría
- comeré → comería
- tendré → tendría
- haré → haría

Then present 4 sentences and ask what the conditional verb communicates:
- "Sería mejor ir mañana." (It would be better...)
- "¿Podrías ayudarme?" (Could you...?)
- "Yo que tú, estudiaría más." (If I were you, I'd...)
- "Dijo que vendría." (He said he would come.)

Guide the learner to notice: hypothetical, polite, advice, reported speech.

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
Conjugation drill leveraging the shared stems with C-03. For each infinitive, produce both the future and the conditional:

1. tener (yo): tendré / tendría
2. hacer (ella): hará / haría
3. poder (nosotros): podremos / podríamos
4. decir (tú): dirás / dirías
5. salir (ellos): saldrán / saldrían
6. venir (yo): vendré / vendría

Then transform sentences by use: "Make this a polite request: 'Puedes ayudarme' → '¿Podrías ayudarme?'" / "Make this advice: 'Habla con ella' → 'Yo que tú, hablaría con ella.'"

### Stage 3: Guided Production
Prompt: "What would you do with a million dollars? Where would you live? What would you buy? Who would you help?"

Listen for correct conditional forms, especially irregulars (haría, pondría, tendría). Scaffold with sentence starters if needed: "Con un millón de dólares, yo..." Then reduce scaffolding: "Tell me about your dream life -- where, what, how, with whom."

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Prompt: "If you could live anywhere in the world, where would you live and why? Describe your ideal life -- your home, your work, your daily routine."

This naturally requires sustained conditional use: viviría, trabajaría, comería, haría, tendría. The focus is on meaning (describing an ideal life) while conditional forms are practiced in context. Follow up: "Give me advice about MY Spanish learning. What should I do? What would you recommend?"

## Dialect Notes
The conditional conjugation is identical across all Spanish dialects. There are no regional differences in the forms.

Usage notes:
- In **Mexican Spanish**, "yo que tú" + conditional is common for advice. The alternative "si yo fuera tú" + conditional is also used but is slightly more formal.
- The polite conditional (¿Podría...?, ¿Tendría...?) is expected in professional and service contexts across all dialects.
- In some regions, the imperfect indicative is used where the conditional might be expected in formal grammar: "Quería pedirte un favor" (I wanted to ask you a favor) functions similarly to "Querría pedirte un favor" -- both are polite, but the imperfect is more common in speech.

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Correctly conjugates regular and all 12 irregular conditional forms. Can produce both future and conditional from the same stem without confusing the endings. Distinguishes conditional from imperfect in contrastive drills.
- **Unscaffolded:** Uses conditional naturally for polite requests, hypothetical statements, and advice in conversation. Chooses conditional over indicative when discussing hypothetical scenarios.
- **Integrated:** Maintains correct conditional forms alongside subjunctive, future, and past tenses without confusion. Uses "yo que tú" + conditional for advice and "dijo que" + conditional for reported speech naturally.

## Connection Points
The conditional is half of the hypothetical system. The other half is the past subjunctive (D-01). Together, they form the core of advanced Spanish expression.

- **C-03** (formal future) shares all 12 irregular stems. The connection between future and conditional should be reinforced: future = what WILL happen, conditional = what WOULD happen.
- **D-01** (past subjunctive) introduces the other half of hypothetical speech. Once D-01 is acquired, si clauses become possible.
- **D-02** (si clauses) pairs conditional with past subjunctive: "Si tuviera dinero, viajaría." (If I had money, I would travel.) This is the target construction that C-04 enables.
- **C-08** (indirect speech) uses the conditional for reported future: "Dijo que vendría" follows the tense-shift rule (future → conditional in past context).

Common regression trigger: when past subjunctive (D-01) is introduced, learners may confuse conditional endings (-ía) with imperfect subjunctive endings (-iera/-iese). Contrastive drills between conditional and past subjunctive should be standard during D-01.
