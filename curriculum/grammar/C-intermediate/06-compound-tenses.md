# Present Perfect and Compound Tenses — Building with Haber

## Overview
Compound tenses combine the auxiliary verb **haber** with a past participle to express completed actions in relation to different time frames. The present perfect ("I have eaten") and pluperfect ("I had eaten") are the workhorses; the future perfect and conditional perfect appear less frequently but complete the system. This concept gives the learner a new dimension of temporal precision.

## When to Teach
- Phase: C
- Prerequisites: B-01-preterite-regular
- L1 interference to preempt: none listed in l1-interference.yaml

## The Pattern

### The Formula

Every compound tense follows one structure:

**conjugated form of haber + past participle (invariable)**

The past participle does NOT change for gender or number in compound tenses. It always ends in -o.

### Past Participle Formation

| Verb Type | Rule | Example |
|-----------|------|---------|
| **-ar verbs** | stem + **-ado** | hablar → habl**ado** |
| **-er verbs** | stem + **-ido** | comer → com**ido** |
| **-ir verbs** | stem + **-ido** | vivir → viv**ido** |

**Accent rule:** When the stem of an -er or -ir verb ends in a vowel, the participle takes an accent: leer → le**ído**, traer → tra**ído**, oír → o**ído**, creer → cre**ído**.

### Irregular Past Participles

These must be memorized. There are no shortcuts.

| Infinitive | Participle | Translation |
|-----------|------------|-------------|
| decir | **dicho** | said |
| hacer | **hecho** | done/made |
| escribir | **escrito** | written |
| ver | **visto** | seen |
| poner | **puesto** | put/placed |
| romper | **roto** | broken |
| abrir | **abierto** | opened |
| volver | **vuelto** | returned |
| morir | **muerto** | died/dead |
| cubrir | **cubierto** | covered |

**Derived forms follow the same pattern:** describir → descrito, devolver → devuelto, descubrir → descubierto, componer → compuesto, resolver → resuelto.

### The Four Compound Tenses

#### Present Perfect (he + participle)

The most important compound tense. Expresses actions completed recently or with present relevance.

| Person | Haber | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| yo | **he** | He comido. |
| tú | **has** | Has comido. |
| él/ella/usted | **ha** | Ha comido. |
| nosotros | **hemos** | Hemos comido. |
| vosotros | **habéis** | Habéis comido. |
| ellos/ustedes | **han** | Han comido. |

#### Pluperfect (había + participle)

Expresses an action completed before another past action. English "had done."

| Person | Haber | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| yo | **había** | Había comido. |
| tú | **habías** | Habías comido. |
| él/ella/usted | **había** | Había comido. |
| nosotros | **habíamos** | Habíamos comido. |
| vosotros | **habíais** | Habíais comido. |
| ellos/ustedes | **habían** | Habían comido. |

#### Future Perfect (habré + participle)

Expresses an action that will be completed before a future point. Also used for probability about the past.

| Person | Haber | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| yo | **habré** | Habré comido. |
| tú | **habrás** | Habrás comido. |
| él/ella/usted | **habrá** | Habrá comido. |
| nosotros | **habremos** | Habremos comido. |
| vosotros | **habréis** | Habréis comido. |
| ellos/ustedes | **habrán** | Habrán comido. |

#### Conditional Perfect (habría + participle)

Expresses what would have happened. Central to hypothetical past speech.

| Person | Haber | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| yo | **habría** | Habría comido. |
| tú | **habrías** | Habrías comido. |
| él/ella/usted | **habría** | Habría comido. |
| nosotros | **habríamos** | Habríamos comido. |
| vosotros | **habríais** | Habríais comido. |
| ellos/ustedes | **habrían** | Habrían comido. |

### The Unbreakable Rule

**Nothing can come between haber and the participle.** In English you can say "I have already eaten" -- the adverb splits the construction. In Spanish, adverbs go before haber or after the participle, never between them:

- "Ya **he comido**." (correct -- adverb before haber)
- "**He comido** ya." (correct -- adverb after participle)
- "**He** ya **comido**." (WRONG -- nothing separates haber from participle)

Object pronouns also precede haber, never splitting the pair:

- "**Lo he hecho.**" (I have done it.) -- correct
- "**He hecho**lo." (WRONG)

## Examples in Context

1. **He visitado** México tres veces. (I have visited Mexico three times.) -- present perfect
2. Cuando llegué, ella ya **había salido**. (When I arrived, she had already left.) -- pluperfect
3. ¿**Has visto** la nueva película? (Have you seen the new movie?) -- present perfect
4. Nunca **habíamos comido** comida tan picante. (We had never eaten such spicy food.) -- pluperfect
5. Para las cinco, ya **habré terminado**. (By five, I will have finished.) -- future perfect
6. **Habría ido** contigo, pero estaba enfermo. (I would have gone with you, but I was sick.) -- conditional perfect
7. ¿Quién **ha roto** el vaso? (Who has broken the glass?) -- present perfect, irregular participle
8. Ellos **habían escrito** la carta antes de la reunión. (They had written the letter before the meeting.) -- pluperfect, irregular participle
9. No **he dicho** nada. (I haven't said anything.) -- present perfect, irregular participle
10. Todavía no **hemos abierto** los regalos. (We still haven't opened the gifts.) -- present perfect, irregular participle

## L1 Interference

**The core problem is structural, not conceptual.** English speakers understand compound tenses well -- "I have eaten" maps directly to "He comido." The errors tend to be mechanical rather than conceptual.

**Common L1-driven mistakes:**

| English Instinct | Wrong | Correct | Why |
|-----------------|-------|---------|-----|
| Split the verb ("I have already eaten") | He ya comido. | Ya he comido. | Nothing separates haber + participle |
| Regular participle for irregular verb | He hacido. | He hecho. | Irregular participles must be memorized |
| Agreement on the participle | Ella ha comida. | Ella ha comido. | Participle is invariable in compound tenses |
| Use present perfect like English | He comido hoy. (in Latin America) | Comí hoy. | Latin America prefers preterite for recent past |

**Preemption script:** "Good news -- compound tenses work a lot like English. 'He comido' is exactly 'I have eaten.' The two things to watch: never put anything between haber and the participle, and memorize the ten irregular participles. Once you have those, the system is very regular."

## Common Errors

1. **Splitting haber and the participle.** "He ya terminado" instead of "Ya he terminado." English freely places adverbs between auxiliary and participle. Spanish treats haber + participle as an inseparable unit. -- L1 interference. Drill word order explicitly.
2. **Regularizing irregular participles.** "He escribido" instead of "He escrito," or "He ponido" instead of "He puesto." -- Developmental. Drill the ten irregular participles until automatic. Flash cards help.
3. **Agreeing the participle with the subject.** "Ella ha comida" instead of "Ella ha comido." This is reinforced by the fact that participles DO agree when used as adjectives (la puerta abierta). -- L1 interference / overgeneralization. Emphasize: in compound tenses, always -o.
4. **Using present perfect where preterite is expected.** In Latin American Spanish, "He comido hoy" sounds unnatural for "I ate today" -- the preterite "Comí hoy" is preferred. -- Dialect mismatch. Teach the regional difference explicitly.
5. **Confusing haber with tener.** "Tengo comido" instead of "He comido." In some regional/archaic Spanish, tener + participle exists, but with different meaning. Standard Spanish uses haber for compound tenses. -- Developmental.

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Present five pairs of sentences -- one in preterite, one in present perfect. Ask the learner to identify what is different and when each version might be preferred:

- "Comí en ese restaurante." / "He comido en ese restaurante."
- "Viajé a España." / "He viajado a España."
- "Escribió tres libros." / "Ha escrito tres libros."
- "No vieron la película." / "No han visto la película."
- "¿Terminaste?" / "¿Has terminado?"

Guide toward noticing: both describe past actions, but the compound form connects to the present or expresses life experience ("Have you ever...?").

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
Two drill formats:

**Conjugation drill:** Give infinitive + subject, learner produces compound form. "Tú / comer (present perfect)" → "Has comido." Cover all persons and mix regular and irregular participles.

**Irregular participle quiz:** Rapid-fire: "decir?" → "dicho." "ver?" → "visto." "hacer?" → "hecho." All ten must become automatic.

### Stage 3: Guided Production
Prompt: "Tell me about your experiences. Use '¿Has...alguna vez?' (Have you ever...?) to ask me questions, and I'll ask you the same. Then tell me about a time when something had already happened before something else (pluperfect)."

Example exchange:
- "¿Has viajado a otro país?" -- "Sí, he viajado a México."
- "Cuando llegué al aeropuerto, ya había salido el avión."

Scaffold by providing the structure, then reduce prompting as the learner gains confidence.

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Prompt: "Tell me about three important things you have done in your life and how they changed you. Then tell me about a time when you arrived too late -- something had already happened."

This naturally elicits present perfect (life experiences) and pluperfect (prior completion), the two highest-frequency compound tenses. Let the learner talk freely, noting compound tense errors for post-conversation review.

## Dialect Notes
The most significant dialect split in compound tenses is the **present perfect vs preterite for recent past:**

- **Spain:** "¿Qué **has hecho** hoy?" (What have you done today?) -- present perfect for actions within the current time frame (today, this week, this year).
- **Latin America (including Mexico):** "¿Qué **hiciste** hoy?" -- preterite for the same question. The present perfect is used mainly for life experiences and ongoing relevance.

Since the target dialect is **Mexican Spanish**, teach the Latin American pattern as default:
- Recent past → preterite ("Comí hace una hora.")
- Life experience / ongoing relevance → present perfect ("He visitado México tres veces.")

The pluperfect, future perfect, and conditional perfect do not vary by dialect.

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Correctly conjugates haber in all four compound tenses. Produces all ten irregular participles without hesitation. Places adverbs and pronouns correctly (outside the haber-participle pair).
- **Unscaffolded:** Uses present perfect naturally for life experience ("He viajado a...") and pluperfect for sequenced past events ("Cuando llegué, ya había salido"). Does not split haber from participle.
- **Integrated:** Chooses correctly between preterite and present perfect based on context (dialect-appropriate). Uses pluperfect alongside preterite and imperfect to narrate complex sequences. Handles irregular participles automatically even in fast speech.

## Connection Points
Compound tenses build on and integrate with several other concepts:

- **B-01** (preterite regular) is the prerequisite. The learner must be solid on simple past before adding the compound past layer.
- **B-04** (preterite vs imperfect) gains a third layer: the pluperfect adds "had already done" to the narrative toolkit.
- **C-05** (por vs para) intersects with passive compound forms: "Ha sido escrito por..." (It has been written by...).
- **D-02** (si clauses) uses the conditional perfect extensively: "Si hubiera sabido, habría ido." (If I had known, I would have gone.)
- **D-03** (subjunctive across all tenses) introduces the present perfect subjunctive (haya comido) and pluperfect subjunctive (hubiera comido).

Common regression trigger: when D-02 introduces "si hubiera + pluperfect subjunctive, habría + conditional perfect," the learner may confuse the two compound forms. Revisit the haber paradigms during D-02 introduction.
