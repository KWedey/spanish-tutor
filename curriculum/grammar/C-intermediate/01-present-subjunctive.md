# Present Subjunctive — A New Mood

## Overview
The subjunctive is not a tense — it is an entirely new **mood**. While indicative states facts about reality, subjunctive expresses desires, doubts, emotions, and unreality. This is the B2 gateway concept: everything in Phase C and D builds on it. Mastering the forms here enables the trigger system in C-02 and unlocks all advanced subjunctive work.

## When to Teach
- Phase: C
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular, A-07-present-irregular-common
- Soft prerequisite: B-04-preterite-vs-imperfect (aspect awareness). While not strictly required for subjunctive forms, a learner who has internalized the preterite/imperfect distinction (completed vs. ongoing) has already practiced thinking about verb meaning beyond tense. This "aspect awareness" transfers directly to mood selection: just as the learner learned to ask "is this completed or ongoing?" for past tense, they now ask "is this real or unreal?" for mood. Learners who skip B-04 can still learn subjunctive, but they tend to struggle more with the conceptual shift because they haven't practiced selecting verb forms based on meaning rather than time.
- L1 interference to preempt: subjunctive-avoidance (HIGH severity)

## The Pattern

### What Is Mood?

Spanish has three moods:
- **Indicative** — states facts: "Ella **habla** español." (She speaks Spanish.)
- **Subjunctive** — expresses desire, doubt, emotion, unreality: "Quiero que ella **hable** español." (I want her to speak Spanish.)
- **Imperative** — gives commands (already learned in B-09)

The subjunctive almost always appears in a subordinate clause after **que**. The structure is:

**[trigger expression] + que + [subjunctive verb]**

### Formation: The "Opposite Vowel" Rule

The key to forming the present subjunctive is simple: **swap the characteristic vowel**.

- **-ar verbs** take **-e** endings (the -er/-ir vowel)
- **-er/-ir verbs** take **-a** endings (the -ar vowel)

### Regular -ar Verbs (hablar)

| Person | Indicative | Subjunctive |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| yo | habl**o** | habl**e** |
| tú | habl**as** | habl**es** |
| él/ella/usted | habl**a** | habl**e** |
| nosotros | habl**amos** | habl**emos** |
| vosotros | habl**áis** | habl**éis** |
| ellos/ustedes | habl**an** | habl**en** |

### Regular -er/-ir Verbs (comer / vivir)

| Person | Indicative | Subjunctive |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| yo | com**o** / viv**o** | com**a** / viv**a** |
| tú | com**es** / viv**es** | com**as** / viv**as** |
| él/ella/usted | com**e** / viv**e** | com**a** / viv**a** |
| nosotros | com**emos** / viv**imos** | com**amos** / viv**amos** |
| vosotros | com**éis** / viv**ís** | com**áis** / viv**áis** |
| ellos/ustedes | com**en** / viv**en** | com**an** / viv**an** |

### Stem-Changing Verbs

Stem changes from the indicative carry into the subjunctive. The same persons change (all except nosotros/vosotros for -ar and -er):

| Infinitive | Subjunctive | Pattern |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| querer (e→ie) | quiera, quieras, quiera, queramos, queráis, quieran | e→ie |
| poder (o→ue) | pueda, puedas, pueda, podamos, podáis, puedan | o→ue |
| dormir (o→ue) | duerma, duermas, duerma, durmamos, durmáis, duerman | o→ue, o→u |
| sentir (e→ie) | sienta, sientas, sienta, sintamos, sintáis, sientan | e→ie, e→i |
| pedir (e→i) | pida, pidas, pida, pidamos, pidáis, pidan | e→i |

**Special nosotros rule for -ir stem-changers:** The nosotros and vosotros forms get the SECOND stem change (the one from preterite): o→u (durmamos) and e→i (sintamos, pidamos). This does not happen with -ar or -er stem-changers.

### Irregular Subjunctive: DISHES

Six verbs have completely irregular subjunctive stems. Use the mnemonic **DISHES**:

| Verb | Subjunctive | Full Paradigm |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| **D**ar | dé | dé, des, dé, demos, deis, den |
| **I**r | vaya | vaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayáis, vayan |
| **S**er | sea | sea, seas, sea, seamos, seáis, sean |
| **H**aber | haya | haya, hayas, haya, hayamos, hayáis, hayan |
| **E**star | esté | esté, estés, esté, estemos, estéis, estén |
| **S**aber | sepa | sepa, sepas, sepa, sepamos, sepáis, sepan |

Note: Dar's yo and usted forms (dé) carry an accent mark to distinguish from the preposition "de." Estar carries accent marks on all forms except nosotros (estemos).

### A Taste of When (Full Triggers in C-02)

The subjunctive appears after expressions of:
- **Desire:** Quiero que..., Espero que..., Prefiero que...
- **Emotion:** Me alegra que..., Siento que..., Me sorprende que...
- **Doubt:** Dudo que..., No creo que...
- **Impersonal expressions:** Es importante que..., Es necesario que...

## Examples in Context

1. Quiero **que hables** más despacio. (I want you to speak more slowly.)
2. Es importante **que estudies** todos los días. (It's important that you study every day.)
3. Espero **que tengas** un buen día. (I hope you have a good day.)
4. Dudo **que sepa** la respuesta. (I doubt he knows the answer.)
5. Me alegra **que estés** aquí. (I'm glad you're here.)
6. Necesito **que vayas** al supermercado. (I need you to go to the supermarket.)
7. Es posible **que llueva** mañana. (It's possible that it rains tomorrow.)
8. Mi mamá quiere **que comamos** en casa. (My mom wants us to eat at home.)
9. No creo **que sea** difícil. (I don't think it's difficult.)
10. Ojalá **que puedas** venir a la fiesta. (I hope you can come to the party.)

## L1 Interference

**English habit:** English rarely uses the subjunctive. Where Spanish says "Quiero que **vengas**," English says "I want you **to come**" — no mood change, no special verb form. Your instinct will be to use indicative everywhere. The subjunctive isn't optional — it's required in specific contexts, and using indicative sounds wrong to native speakers.

**The core problem:** English speakers process "I want you to come" as a simple statement and reach for the indicative "vienes." They must learn that the "que" clause after a trigger demands a different verb form — the subjunctive.

**Common L1-driven mistakes:**

| English | Instinct (wrong) | Correct | Why |
|---------|------------------|---------|-----|
| I want you to come. | Quiero que **vienes**. | Quiero que **vengas**. | Desire + que = subjunctive |
| I hope it rains. | Espero que **llueve**. | Espero que **llueva**. | Hope + que = subjunctive |
| It's important that he studies. | Es importante que **estudia**. | Es importante que **estudie**. | Impersonal + que = subjunctive |
| I doubt she knows. | Dudo que **sabe**. | Dudo que **sepa**. | Doubt + que = subjunctive (irregular) |

**Preemption script:** "Everything you've learned so far has been in the indicative mood — stating facts, describing reality. Now we're entering a completely different territory. The subjunctive is a new mood, and it's used when you're talking about things that aren't facts yet — wishes, doubts, emotions, possibilities. English barely has a subjunctive, so your brain will want to use the regular indicative forms. That's totally normal. The key is recognizing the trigger words — quiero que, espero que, dudo que — and switching to the 'opposite vowel' form. We'll drill this until it becomes automatic."

## Common Errors

1. **Using indicative after subjunctive triggers.** "Quiero que vienes" instead of "Quiero que vengas." The single most common error. Every trigger expression + que demands the subjunctive. — L1 interference. Drill trigger + subjunctive pairs until the pattern is automatic.
2. **Wrong "opposite vowel" direction.** "Quiero que habla" (no change) or "Quiero que comer" (infinitive instead of subjunctive). The learner knows a change is needed but applies it incorrectly. — developmental. Reinforce: -ar→-e, -er/-ir→-a.
3. **Irregular form errors.** "Quiero que saba" (wrong stem for saber) or "Es importante que va" (missing irregular stem for ir). The DISHES irregulars must be memorized. — developmental. Drill the six irregulars explicitly.
4. **Forgetting the nosotros stem change for -ir verbs.** "Quiere que sentamos" instead of "Quiere que sintamos." The nosotros -ir change (e→i, o→u) is easy to miss. — developmental. Highlight this as a special case during conjugation drills.
5. **Using subjunctive in the main clause.** "Quiera que vengas" instead of "Quiero que vengas." Only the subordinate clause (after que) takes the subjunctive. The main clause stays indicative. — overgeneralization. Clarify the two-clause structure.

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Present indicative/subjunctive sentence pairs side by side. Ask the learner what is different about the verb forms:

- "Ella habla español." vs "Quiero que ella hable español."
- "Tú comes mucho." vs "Es importante que tú comas bien."
- "Él va al mercado." vs "Necesito que él vaya al mercado."

Guide the learner to notice: (1) the second sentence has a trigger + que, (2) the verb form changes, (3) the vowel seems to flip. Don't explain the full rule yet — let them articulate what they see.

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
Conjugation drill: give the infinitive and subject, learner produces the subjunctive form. Start with regulars, add stem-changers, then irregulars.

1. hablar (yo) → hable
2. comer (tú) → comas
3. vivir (nosotros) → vivamos
4. querer (ella) → quiera
5. ir (ustedes) → vayan
6. ser (yo) → sea
7. dormir (nosotros) → durmamos
8. saber (tú) → sepas

Then frame them in sentences: "Quiero que [tú / hablar más]" → "Quiero que hables más."

### Stage 3: Guided Production
Prompt: "Tell me what you want other people to do. Your boss, your family, your friends. Use 'Quiero que...' or 'Necesito que...' or 'Espero que...'"

Listen for correct subjunctive forms. Scaffold by providing the trigger phrase if needed, then gradually remove the scaffold. If the learner uses indicative after que, recast: "Quiero que vienes? — Quiero que vengas. Right, after 'quiero que' we use the subjunctive."

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Prompt: "Give me advice about learning Spanish. What's important? What do you recommend? Use phrases like 'Es importante que...', 'Recomiendo que...', 'Es mejor que...'"

This naturally requires subjunctive after impersonal expressions and recommendation triggers. Focus is on meaning (genuine advice) while the subjunctive forms get practiced in context.

## Dialect Notes
The present subjunctive forms are universal across all Spanish dialects. There are no regional differences in the conjugation itself.

The vosotros forms (habléis, comáis) are Spain-specific. Latin American Spanish uses ustedes forms exclusively, which simplifies the paradigm by one row.

In Mexican Spanish, "ojalá" is extremely common and always triggers subjunctive — a high-frequency entry point for practice.

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Correctly conjugates regular, stem-changing, and irregular subjunctive forms in drills. Gets DISHES irregulars right without hesitation. Produces the "opposite vowel" consistently.
- **Unscaffolded:** Uses subjunctive forms after trigger expressions in conversation without being prompted or corrected. Does not revert to indicative after "quiero que," "es importante que," etc.
- **Integrated:** Maintains subjunctive usage while managing other grammatical complexity (object pronouns, past tenses, compound sentences). Does not regress to indicative when cognitive load increases.

## Connection Points
This is THE B2 gateway concept. Every grammar concept from C-02 through C-09 and all of Phase D depends on subjunctive mastery.

- **C-02** (subjunctive triggers) builds directly on this: once the forms are solid, the full trigger system is introduced.
- **C-07** (relative clauses) uses subjunctive for uncertain/nonexistent antecedents: "Busco alguien que hable inglés."
- **C-08** (indirect speech) requires subjunctive for reported commands: "Me pidió que viniera."
- **D-01** (past subjunctive) applies these same patterns with past-tense stems.
- **D-02** (si clauses) pairs past subjunctive with conditional for hypotheticals.
- **D-03** (subjunctive across all tenses) integrates everything.

Expect this concept to take weeks to feel natural. The forms can be drilled to competence relatively quickly, but automatic production in conversation takes sustained practice. The decision engine should continue cycling this concept long after initial introduction.

Common regression trigger: when past subjunctive (D-01) is introduced, learners sometimes confuse present and past subjunctive forms. Revisit this concept explicitly if regression is detected.
