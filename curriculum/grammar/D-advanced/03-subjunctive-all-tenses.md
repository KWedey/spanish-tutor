# Subjunctive Across All Tenses — The Complete System

## Overview
This is an integration concept, not a new form. The learner already knows present subjunctive (C-01), past subjunctive (D-01), and compound tenses (C-06). This concept weaves them together into a unified system governed by the **sequence of tenses** — the rule that the main clause tense determines which subjunctive tense appears in the subordinate clause. Mastery here means the learner can express subjunctive meaning in any time frame without hesitation.

## When to Teach
- Phase: D
- Prerequisites: D-01-past-subjunctive, C-06-compound-tenses
- L1 interference to preempt: none specific (this is a Spanish-internal integration challenge)

## The Pattern

### The Four Subjunctive Tenses

| Tense | Formation | Example | English |
|-------|-----------|---------|---------|
| Present subjunctive | opposite vowel (C-01) | **hable** | speak / may speak |
| Imperfect subjunctive | preterite stem + -ra/-se (D-01) | **hablara** | spoke / might speak |
| Present perfect subjunctive | haya + participle | **haya hablado** | has spoken / may have spoken |
| Pluperfect subjunctive | hubiera + participle | **hubiera hablado** | had spoken / might have spoken |

### Formation of the Two Compound Subjunctive Tenses

#### Present Perfect Subjunctive (haya + participle)

Haber in the present subjunctive + past participle:

| Person | Form | Example |
|--------|------|---------|
| yo | **haya** hablado | haya comido, haya vivido |
| tú | **hayas** hablado | hayas comido, hayas vivido |
| él/ella/usted | **haya** hablado | haya comido, haya vivido |
| nosotros | **hayamos** hablado | hayamos comido, hayamos vivido |
| vosotros | **hayáis** hablado | hayáis comido, hayáis vivido |
| ellos/ustedes | **hayan** hablado | hayan comido, hayan vivido |

#### Pluperfect Subjunctive (hubiera + participle)

Haber in the past subjunctive + past participle:

| Person | Form | Example |
|--------|------|---------|
| yo | **hubiera** hablado | hubiera comido, hubiera vivido |
| tú | **hubieras** hablado | hubieras comido, hubieras vivido |
| él/ella/usted | **hubiera** hablado | hubiera comido, hubiera vivido |
| nosotros | **hubiéramos** hablado | hubiéramos comido, hubiéramos vivido |
| vosotros | **hubierais** hablado | hubierais comido, hubierais vivido |
| ellos/ustedes | **hubieran** hablado | hubieran comido, hubieran vivido |

Note: The -se variants also exist (hubiese hablado) but are rare in speech.

### The Sequence of Tenses

The tense of the **main clause** determines which subjunctive tense appears in the **subordinate clause**. This is the governing rule of the entire system.

#### Rule 1: Present / Future Main Clause --> Present Subjunctive

When the main verb is present, future, or present perfect:

- **Quiero** que **vengas**. (I want you to come.)
- **Espero** que **tengas** un buen día. (I hope you have a good day.)
- **Es importante** que **estudies**. (It's important that you study.)

#### Rule 2: Past Main Clause --> Imperfect Subjunctive

When the main verb is preterite, imperfect, conditional, or pluperfect:

- **Quería** que **vinieras**. (I wanted you to come.)
- **Era importante** que **estudiaras**. (It was important that you study.)
- **Me pidió** que **hablara** despacio. (She asked me to speak slowly.)
- **Dudaba** que **supiera** la respuesta. (I doubted he knew the answer.)

#### Rule 3: Present Main + Completed Action --> Present Perfect Subjunctive

When the main verb is present, but the subordinate action is completed:

- **Me alegro** de que **hayas venido**. (I'm glad you've come.)
- **No creo** que **haya terminado**. (I don't think he has finished.)
- **Es bueno** que **hayamos hablado**. (It's good that we have talked.)
- **Espero** que **hayas dormido** bien. (I hope you slept well.)

#### Rule 4: Past Main + Earlier Action --> Pluperfect Subjunctive

When the main verb is past, and the subordinate action happened even earlier:

- **Dudaba** que **hubiera llegado** a tiempo. (I doubted he had arrived on time.)
- **No creía** que **hubieran terminado**. (I didn't believe they had finished.)
- **Me sorprendió** que **hubiera dicho** eso. (It surprised me that he had said that.)
- **Quería** que **hubiéramos salido** antes. (He wanted us to have left earlier.)

### The Complete Map

| Main clause tense | Simultaneous / future action | Completed / prior action |
|-------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Present / future | present subjunctive | present perfect subjunctive |
| Past / conditional | imperfect subjunctive | pluperfect subjunctive |

This is the entire system in one table. Every subjunctive choice flows from two questions: (1) Is the main clause present or past? (2) Is the subordinate action simultaneous/future or completed/prior?

### Ojalá: A Special Case

Ojalá behaves differently depending on which subjunctive follows:

| Ojalá + | Meaning | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| present subjunctive | hope (possible) | Ojalá **llueva**. (I hope it rains — it might.) |
| imperfect subjunctive | wish (unlikely) | Ojalá **lloviera**. (I wish it would rain — it probably won't.) |
| present perfect subjunctive | hope about completed | Ojalá **haya llegado**. (I hope she has arrived — I don't know yet.) |
| pluperfect subjunctive | regret (impossible) | Ojalá **hubiera llovido**. (I wish it had rained — it didn't.) |

## Examples in Context

1. **Quiero** que **vengas** a mi fiesta. (I want you to come to my party.) — present + present subj
2. **Quería** que **vinieras** a mi fiesta. (I wanted you to come to my party.) — past + imperfect subj
3. **Me alegro** de que **hayas venido**. (I'm glad you've come.) — present + present perfect subj
4. **Dudaba** que **hubiera llegado** a tiempo. (I doubted he had arrived on time.) — past + pluperfect subj
5. **Es necesario** que **estudien** más. (It's necessary that they study more.) — present + present subj
6. **Era necesario** que **estudiaran** más. (It was necessary that they study more.) — past + imperfect subj
7. **Espero** que **hayan pasado** bien las vacaciones. (I hope you've had a good vacation.) — present + present perfect subj
8. No **creía** que **hubieran podido** hacerlo. (I didn't believe they had been able to do it.) — past + pluperfect subj
9. Ojalá **pueda** ir mañana. (I hope I can go tomorrow.) — hope, possible
10. Ojalá **hubiera podido** ir. (I wish I had been able to go.) — regret, impossible

## L1 Interference

**English does not have a sequence-of-tenses rule for subjunctive.** English barely distinguishes subjunctive tenses at all. "I want you to come" and "I wanted you to come" use the same infinitive form ("to come"). The learner must learn that Spanish shifts the subjunctive tense to match the main clause — a system that has no English parallel.

**Common L1-driven mistakes:**

| English | Instinct (wrong) | Correct | Why |
|---------|------------------|---------|-----|
| I wanted you to come. | Quería que **vengas**. | Quería que **vinieras**. | Past main = past subjunctive |
| I'm glad you've come. | Me alegro de que **vienes**. | Me alegro de que **hayas venido**. | Completed action = perfect subjunctive |
| I doubted he had arrived. | Dudaba que **había llegado**. | Dudaba que **hubiera llegado**. | Past main + prior action = pluperfect subjunctive |

**Preemption script:** "You already know present subjunctive and past subjunctive as separate concepts. Now we're connecting them into a system. The rule is simple: look at the main verb. Is it present? Use present subjunctive. Is it past? Use past subjunctive. Has the subordinate action already been completed? Use the perfect form. That's the whole system — two questions, four possibilities."

## Common Errors

1. **Using present subjunctive after past main clause.** "Quería que vengas" instead of "Quería que vinieras." The learner defaults to the more familiar present subjunctive regardless of main clause tense. — developmental. Drill the contrast: Quiero que vengas / Quería que vinieras.
2. **Using indicative instead of compound subjunctive.** "Me alegro de que has venido" instead of "Me alegro de que hayas venido." The learner recognizes the compound structure but forgets that it must be subjunctive after triggers. — L1 interference. Reinforce: after a subjunctive trigger, ALL clauses with "que" take subjunctive, including compound forms.
3. **Avoiding compound subjunctive entirely.** The learner simplifies: "Me alegro de que vengas" (present subjunctive) when the context requires "Me alegro de que hayas venido" (present perfect subjunctive for a completed action). — avoidance. Specifically practice scenarios where the action has already happened.
4. **Confusing pluperfect subjunctive with pluperfect indicative.** "Dudaba que había llegado" instead of "Dudaba que hubiera llegado." Both use haber + participle, but the subjunctive requires hubiera, not había. — developmental. Drill the contrast: sabía que había llegado (I knew he had arrived — indicative, fact) vs dudaba que hubiera llegado (I doubted he had arrived — subjunctive, doubt).
5. **Mixing up ojalá meanings.** Using "Ojalá llueva" (hope, possible) when intending "Ojalá lloviera" (wish, unlikely). The subjunctive tense after ojalá changes the entire meaning. — developmental. Practice with clear context: "It's a sunny day and you wish for rain" vs "The forecast says maybe rain."

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Present four sentences with the same trigger but different time references. Ask the learner to identify what changes and why:

- "Quiero que vengas." (now, looking forward)
- "Quería que vinieras." (past)
- "Me alegro de que hayas venido." (now, looking back at completed action)
- "Dudaba que hubiera venido." (past, looking further back)

Guide toward noticing: the main verb tense and the timing of the subordinate action determine which subjunctive form appears. Let the learner articulate the pattern before presenting the two-question framework.

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
Two drills:

**Sequence drill:** Give a present-tense sentence, learner shifts the entire sentence to past:
1. Quiero que vengas --> Quería que vinieras.
2. Espero que sepas --> Esperaba que supieras.
3. Me alegro de que hayas terminado --> Me alegraba de que hubieras terminado.
4. Es importante que estudien --> Era importante que estudiaran.

**Two-question drill:** Give a scenario, learner identifies (1) main clause tense and (2) simultaneous or completed, then produces the correct form:
- "You're glad right now that your friend arrived an hour ago." --> present + completed = Me alegro de que hayas llegado.
- "You doubted yesterday that she had finished the day before." --> past + prior = Dudaba que hubiera terminado.

### Stage 3: Guided Production
Prompt: "Let's talk about your week. Tell me things you're glad about that have happened (Me alegro de que...), things you hoped for (Esperaba que...), and things you wish had gone differently (Ojalá hubiera...)."

This naturally elicits present perfect subjunctive (glad about completed events), imperfect subjunctive (past hopes), and pluperfect subjunctive (regrets). Scaffold with the trigger phrases, then gradually remove them.

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Prompt: "Tell me the story of a big decision in your life. What did people want you to do? (Querían que...) What were you glad about afterward? (Me alegré de que...) What do you wish had been different? (Ojalá hubiera...)"

This requires fluid movement between all four subjunctive tenses within a single narrative. The focus is on telling the story (meaning) while the subjunctive forms are practiced in context. After the narrative, do a brief review of 2-3 forms that were difficult.

## Dialect Notes
The subjunctive tense system is identical across all Spanish dialects. There are no regional differences in the sequence-of-tenses rules.

Usage notes:
- In **conversational Latin American Spanish**, the present perfect subjunctive (haya + participle) is less common than in Spain. Where a Spaniard might say "Me alegro de que hayas venido," a Mexican speaker might use the present subjunctive: "Me alegro de que vengas." Both are correct; this parallels the broader present-perfect-vs-preterite split between regions.
- The pluperfect subjunctive is used across all dialects for Type 3 si clauses and for expressing regret with ojalá.
- The -se variants (hubiese hablado) are grammatically interchangeable with -ra forms in all compound subjunctive tenses but are primarily literary.

See `curriculum/dialect-notes.yaml` for regional vocabulary preferences.

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Correctly selects the appropriate subjunctive tense based on main clause tense and action timing. Can produce all four subjunctive forms (present, imperfect, present perfect, pluperfect) on demand in drills. Correctly shifts sentences from present to past context.
- **Unscaffolded:** Naturally matches subjunctive tense to main clause tense in conversation. Uses present perfect subjunctive for completed actions and pluperfect subjunctive for regrets without prompting. Does not default to present subjunctive for all contexts.
- **Integrated:** Maintains correct sequence of tenses while managing complex narratives involving multiple time frames. Uses ojalá with the appropriate subjunctive tense to distinguish hope, wish, and regret. Handles si clauses (D-02) and indirect speech (C-08) with correct subjunctive tense selection simultaneously.

## Connection Points
This concept is the capstone of the subjunctive system.

- **C-01** (present subjunctive) introduced the mood and its triggers. This concept assumes those triggers are well-known.
- **D-01** (past subjunctive) introduced the imperfect subjunctive forms. This concept integrates them with the compound forms.
- **C-06** (compound tenses) provided the haber paradigms. The learner applies those same patterns with subjunctive forms of haber (haya, hubiera).
- **D-02** (si clauses) is the primary consumer of imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive. Sequence-of-tenses mastery here directly supports si clause accuracy.
- **C-02** (subjunctive triggers) remains foundational. The same triggers apply across all four tenses — the trigger does not change, only the tense selection.
- **C-08** (indirect speech) combines tense shifting with subjunctive: "Me dijo que quería que viniera" requires two shifts — indirect speech (present-->past) and subjunctive trigger (que-->subjunctive).

Common regression trigger: the four-way choice can overwhelm. If the learner starts producing only present or only past subjunctive, simplify to the two-question framework: (1) main clause present or past? (2) action simultaneous or completed? Two questions, four answers.
