# Numbers and Quantifiers

## Overview
Numbers and quantifiers are among the most immediately useful vocabulary and grammar a learner encounters — telling your age, asking prices, counting items, expressing quantities. Spanish numbers follow patterns that become predictable after the first 30, but quantifiers introduce a hidden complexity: many of them must agree in gender and number with the noun they modify, just like adjectives. The single most important L1 interference point here is age expressions: English uses "to be" (I am 20 years old), but Spanish uses "tener" (Tengo 20 años). Getting this wrong is an immediate marker of a beginner.

## When to Teach
- Phase: A
- Prerequisites: [A-03-gender-agreement] (quantifiers must agree in gender/number)
- L1 interference to preempt: tener-for-age (severity: high), quantifier-agreement (severity: medium)

## The Pattern

### 1. Cardinal Numbers (0-1000+)

| Range | Pattern | Examples |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **0-15** | Unique words — memorize | cero, uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez, once, doce, trece, catorce, quince |
| **16-19** | dieci- + unit | dieciséis, diecisiete, dieciocho, diecinueve |
| **20-29** | veinti- + unit | veinte, veintiuno, veintidós, veintitrés... veintinueve |
| **30-99** | tens + y + unit | treinta y uno, cuarenta y dos, cincuenta y tres, sesenta y cuatro |
| **100** | cien (alone) / ciento (+ more) | cien personas, ciento veinte personas |
| **200-999** | hundreds agree in gender | doscientos libros / doscientas casas, trescientos / trescientas |
| **1,000+** | mil (invariable) | mil, dos mil, tres mil quinientos |

**Key rules:**
- **Uno** shortens to **un** before masculine nouns: un libro, veintiún años.
- **Una** before feminine nouns: una casa, veintiuna personas.
- **Hundreds (200-999) agree in gender:** doscientos hombres / doscientas mujeres.
- **Mil** does not change: mil casas, dos mil libros.

### 2. Ordinal Numbers (primero-décimo)

| Number | Masculine | Feminine |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| 1st | primero (primer) | primera |
| 2nd | segundo | segunda |
| 3rd | tercero (tercer) | tercera |
| 4th | cuarto | cuarta |
| 5th | quinto | quinta |
| 6th | sexto | sexta |
| 7th | séptimo | séptima |
| 8th | octavo | octava |
| 9th | noveno | novena |
| 10th | décimo | décima |

**Key rules:**
- Ordinals agree in gender and number like adjectives: la primera vez, los primeros días.
- **Primero** and **tercero** drop the -o before masculine singular nouns: el **primer** libro, el **tercer** piso.
- Beyond décimo, Spanish typically uses cardinal numbers after the noun: el siglo veintiuno, el piso doce.

### 3. Quantifiers That Agree (Gender and Number)

| Quantifier | Masc. sg. | Fem. sg. | Masc. pl. | Fem. pl. | Meaning |
|------------|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|---------|
| mucho | mucho | mucha | muchos | muchas | a lot of, much, many |
| poco | poco | poca | pocos | pocas | a little, few |
| demasiado | demasiado | demasiada | demasiados | demasiadas | too much, too many |
| todo | todo | toda | todos | todas | all, every |
| otro | otro | otra | otros | otras | another, other |
| alguno | algún | alguna | algunos | algunas | some, any |
| ninguno | ningún | ninguna | (ningunos) | (ningunas) | no, none, not any |

**Key rules:**
- **Alguno** and **ninguno** shorten to **algún** and **ningún** before masculine singular nouns.
- **Ninguno** is almost always singular: No tengo **ningún** problema.
- **Todo** requires an article after it: **todos los** días, **toda la** noche.
- **Otro** never takes un/una before it: Quiero **otra** cerveza (NOT *una otra cerveza*).

### 4. Quantifiers That Do NOT Agree

| Quantifier | Example | Meaning |
|------------|---------|---------|
| bastante / bastantes | bastante agua / bastantes libros | enough, quite a lot |
| cada | cada día, cada persona | each, every |
| más | más agua, más libros | more |
| menos | menos tiempo, menos personas | less, fewer |

> **Note:** Bastante only changes for number (singular/plural), not gender. Cada, más, and menos never change.

### 5. Expressions with Tener (NOT Ser)

Spanish uses **tener** (to have) for many states where English uses "to be":

| English (with "to be") | Spanish (with "tener") | Common error |
|------------------------|------------------------|--------------|
| I am 20 years old | **Tengo** 20 años | *~~Soy 20 años~~* |
| I am hungry | **Tengo** hambre | *~~Soy hambriento~~* |
| I am thirsty | **Tengo** sed | *~~Soy sediento~~* |
| I am hot/cold | **Tengo** calor / frío | *~~Soy caliente~~* (means something else!) |
| I am sleepy | **Tengo** sueño | *~~Soy dormido~~* |
| I am afraid | **Tengo** miedo | *~~Soy asustado~~* |
| I am in a hurry | **Tengo** prisa | *~~Soy apurado~~* |

## Examples in Context

1. Tengo **veinticinco** años. (I am twenty-five years old.)
2. Hay **doscientas** personas en la fiesta. (There are two hundred people at the party.)
3. Quiero **otra** taza de café. (I want another cup of coffee.)
4. No tengo **ningún** problema con eso. (I don't have any problem with that.)
5. **Todos los** días estudio español. (Every day I study Spanish.)
6. Hay **pocas** sillas y **muchas** personas. (There are few chairs and many people.)
7. Es el **primer** día del **tercer** mes. (It's the first day of the third month.)
8. **Cada** estudiante tiene **bastantes** libros. (Each student has enough books.)
9. Mi abuela tiene **noventa y dos** años. (My grandmother is ninety-two years old.)
10. Tengo **demasiada** hambre y **poca** paciencia. (I am too hungry and have little patience.)

## L1 Interference

**English habit:** English uses "to be" for age ("I am 20 years old"), hunger ("I am hungry"), and other physical/emotional states. English quantifiers never change form ("many books," "many houses" — "many" stays the same). English says "a hundred" but Spanish uses "cien" alone.

**Spanish reality:** Age and physical states use "tener" (to have), not "ser" or "estar." Quantifiers like mucho/poco/demasiado must agree in gender and number, just like adjectives. Hundreds (200-999) agree in gender.

**Contrast pairs:**

| English instinct | Correct Spanish |
|------------------|-----------------|
| I am 20 years old (to be + age) | **Tengo** 20 años (to have + years) |
| I am hungry (to be + adjective) | **Tengo** hambre (to have + noun) |
| many books / many houses (same "many") | **muchos** libros / **muchas** casas (agrees) |
| two hundred books / two hundred houses | **doscientos** libros / **doscientas** casas (agrees) |

**Preemption script:** "In English you say 'I am 20 years old' — but in Spanish, you literally say 'I have 20 years': Tengo veinte años. Think of age as something you possess, not something you are. This applies to hunger, thirst, fear, and other states too — Spanish treats them as things you have, not things you are."

## Common Errors

1. **Ser for age:** *"Soy veinticinco años"* instead of "Tengo veinticinco años." — L1 interference (high severity). Direct translation from English "I am." This is the single most common beginner error with numbers. Drill tener + age until it becomes automatic.
2. **Quantifier agreement failure:** *"Mucho personas"* instead of "muchas personas." — Developmental. Learner treats mucho as invariable like English "many." Reinforce that mucho/poco/demasiado behave like adjectives.
3. **Un otro:** *"Quiero un otro café"* instead of "Quiero otro café." — L1 interference. Direct translation from "another" = "an other." Drill: otro never takes un/una before it.
4. **Hundreds gender:** *"Doscientos casas"* instead of "doscientas casas." — Developmental. Learner has not yet internalized that hundreds agree in gender. Consistent exposure corrects this.
5. **Cien vs. ciento confusion:** *"Ciento personas"* instead of "cien personas," or *"cien veinte"* instead of "ciento veinte." — Developmental. Rule: cien when alone or before a noun, ciento when followed by more numbers.

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Present number patterns in groups. Show 0-15 as unique words. Then reveal the pattern: "Look at 16-19 — what do you notice? What about 20-29?" Let the learner discover that dieciséis = diez + seis compressed. Show pairs with quantifiers: "muchos libros / muchas casas / mucha agua" — ask what pattern they see. Then present: "I am 20 years old" alongside "Tengo 20 años." Ask: "What verb does Spanish use here?"

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
Three-part drill. Part 1: Number dictation — tutor says a number (in context: prices, ages, addresses), learner writes it in Spanish. Start 1-30, then expand. Part 2: Quantifier agreement — give a quantifier + noun, learner produces the correct form: "mucho / personas" → "muchas personas." Include todo (which needs the article): "todo / días" → "todos los días." Part 3: Tener expressions — tutor describes a situation in English ("You just ran a marathon"), learner responds with the tener expression ("Tengo sed, tengo calor, tengo sueño").

### Stage 3: Guided Production
Prompt: "Tell me about your family — how old is each person? Use at least three quantifiers to describe your family (many, few, each, all, another)." This naturally requires tener + age, quantifier agreement, and number production. Recast errors after the learner finishes each description, not mid-sentence.

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Prompt: "You are at a market in Mexico. Ask about prices, say how many of each item you want, and tell the vendor you need more of something and less of something else." This requires cardinal numbers, quantifiers (más, menos, otro, bastante, demasiado), and natural conversation. Alternative: "Describe the people at a party — how many are there, how old do they look, what do they have too much of or not enough of?"

## Dialect Notes
Cardinal and ordinal numbers are consistent across all major dialects. Minor vocabulary differences: "mil millones" = billion in Latin America (same as European usage), where "billón" = trillion. Currency contexts vary: pesos, euros, soles, etc. The tener expressions are universal across all dialects. Quantifier usage is consistent. See `curriculum/dialect-notes.yaml` for region-specific number expressions (e.g., "cuatrocientos" vs. rare archaic forms).

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Correctly produces numbers 0-100 in drills, uses tener for age when prompted, makes quantifiers agree when attention is on the form.
- **Unscaffolded:** Spontaneously uses "tengo X años" in free conversation without hesitation. Quantifiers agree naturally in free production. Numbers above 100 are produced with correct gender agreement on hundreds.
- **Integrated:** Uses tener expressions for age, hunger, thirst, etc., without any trace of "ser" interference. Quantifier agreement is maintained during complex sentences. Numbers flow naturally in transactional conversations (shopping, directions, scheduling).
