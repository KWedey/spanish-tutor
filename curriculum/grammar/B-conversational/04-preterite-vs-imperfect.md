# Preterite vs Imperfect — Aspect Distinction

## Overview
This is the hardest concept in Phase B and one of the most persistent challenges in all of Spanish. It is NOT about learning new verb forms -- the learner already knows both tenses. It is about understanding that Spanish encodes **aspect** (how an action relates to time) while English does not. The same event, described with preterite or imperfect, carries different meaning. Mastering this distinction is the gateway to B1 fluency.

## When to Teach
- Phase: B
- Prerequisites: B-01-preterite-regular, B-02-preterite-irregular, B-03-imperfect
- L1 interference to preempt: preterite-imperfect-overuse (HIGH severity)

## The Pattern

This concept is about MEANING and ASPECT, not about conjugation. The learner already knows the forms. The question is always: **"What do I mean by this?"**

### The Core Framework

**Preterite — Completed, bounded, foreground:**
- Completed action with a beginning and end
- Specific time or duration
- Sequence of events (first this, then that)
- Change of state or condition
- Reaction or emotional response to an event

**Imperfect — Ongoing, unbounded, background:**
- Ongoing state with no defined endpoint
- Habitual or repeated action in the past
- Background description or scene-setting
- Physical or emotional description
- Simultaneous actions (while X was happening...)
- Age, time, and weather in the past

### Key Contrast Pairs

The same verb can take either tense. The tense changes the MEANING:

| Preterite | Meaning | Imperfect | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Conocí** a María. | I met María. (event -- first encounter) | **Conocía** a María. | I knew María. (ongoing state) |
| **Fue** difícil. | It was difficult. (completed judgment) | **Era** difícil. | It was difficult. (background description) |
| **Supo** la verdad. | He found out the truth. (moment of discovery) | **Sabía** la verdad. | He knew the truth. (ongoing knowledge) |
| **Tuvo** un problema. | He had a problem. (event -- it arose) | **Tenía** un problema. | He had a problem. (ongoing situation) |
| **Pudo** escapar. | He managed to escape. (succeeded) | **Podía** escapar. | He was able to escape. (had the ability) |
| **Quiso** salir. | He tried to leave. (attempted) | **Quería** salir. | He wanted to leave. (ongoing desire) |
| **No quiso** ir. | He refused to go. (active refusal) | **No quería** ir. | He didn't want to go. (state of mind) |

### Both Tenses Together

This is where the distinction comes alive. In natural narration, preterite and imperfect weave together:

**Imperfect sets the scene. Preterite advances the action.**

- Mientras **caminaba** (imperfect -- ongoing), **vi** (preterite -- interruption) a un perro.
  (While I was walking, I saw a dog.)

- **Llovía** (imperfect -- background) cuando **salimos** (preterite -- event) del restaurante.
  (It was raining when we left the restaurant.)

- **Eran** (imperfect -- time) las diez de la noche y todo **estaba** (imperfect -- description) tranquilo. De repente, **sonó** (preterite -- interruption) el teléfono.
  (It was ten at night and everything was quiet. Suddenly, the phone rang.)

## Examples in Context

1. Cuando **era** niño, siempre **jugaba** en el parque. (When I was a child, I always played in the park.) -- imperfect: habitual past + background
2. Ayer **fui** al parque y **jugué** una hora. (Yesterday I went to the park and played for one hour.) -- preterite: completed events with specific time
3. **Hacía** mucho calor y todos **tenían** sed. Entonces mi mamá **compró** agua para todos. (It was very hot and everyone was thirsty. Then my mom bought water for everyone.) -- imperfect for scene, preterite for action
4. Mi abuela siempre **cocinaba** los domingos. Un domingo, **decidió** enseñarme su receta secreta. (My grandmother always cooked on Sundays. One Sunday, she decided to teach me her secret recipe.) -- imperfect for habitual, preterite for specific event
5. **Caminábamos** por la playa cuando **encontramos** una estrella de mar. (We were walking on the beach when we found a starfish.) -- imperfect for ongoing, preterite for interruption
6. **Quería** llamarte, pero no **tuve** tiempo. (I wanted to call you, but I didn't have time.) -- imperfect for ongoing desire, preterite for bounded lack of time
7. La fiesta **fue** increíble. **Había** mucha gente, la música **sonaba** fuerte, y todos **bailaban**. A las doce, **cantamos** "Las Mañanitas". (The party was incredible. There were many people, the music was playing loud, and everyone was dancing. At twelve, we sang "Las Mananitas.") -- preterite for overall assessment, imperfect for scene details, preterite for specific event
8. **Tenía** veinte años cuando **viajé** a México por primera vez. (I was twenty years old when I traveled to Mexico for the first time.) -- imperfect for age, preterite for event
9. No **sabía** que **estabas** aquí. ¿Cuándo **llegaste**? (I didn't know you were here. When did you arrive?) -- imperfect for states, preterite for completed event
10. El examen **fue** difícil, pero **estudié** mucho y **saqué** buena nota. **Estaba** muy contento. (The exam was difficult, but I studied a lot and got a good grade. I was very happy.) -- preterite for completed events, imperfect for resulting emotional state
11. Mientras mi papá **leía** el periódico, mi mamá **preparaba** el desayuno, y yo **veía** la televisión. De repente, se **fue** la luz. (While my dad was reading the newspaper, my mom was preparing breakfast, and I was watching TV. Suddenly, the power went out.) -- imperfect for simultaneous ongoing actions, preterite for interruption

## L1 Interference

**English habit:** English uses one past tense for both "I ate" (completed) and "I was eating" (ongoing). Your instinct will be to use preterite for everything. The imperfect feels unnecessary -- but it carries meaning that preterite can't.

**The core problem:** English speakers process past events as "it happened" and reach for the preterite. They must learn to ask: "Am I describing what happened, or what was happening? An event, or a scene?"

**Common L1-driven mistakes:**

| English | Instinct (wrong) | Correct | Why |
|---------|------------------|---------|-----|
| When I was young... | Cuando **fui** joven... | Cuando **era** joven... | Age is a state, not an event |
| It was raining. | **Llovió.** | **Llovía.** | Background weather, not a completed rain event |
| I wanted to go. | **Quise** ir. | **Quería** ir. | Ongoing desire (quise = I tried to go) |
| She was beautiful. | **Fue** hermosa. | **Era** hermosa. | Description, not a completed state |
| Every day I walked... | Todos los días **caminé**... | Todos los días **caminaba**... | Habitual action, not a single event |

**Preemption script:** "English uses one past tense for both 'I ate dinner' and 'I was eating dinner.' Spanish forces you to choose: preterite for the completed event, imperfect for the ongoing action. When you're telling a story, think of it like a movie -- the imperfect is the background music and scenery, the preterite is the action that moves the plot forward. Your instinct will be to use preterite for everything. That's normal. We'll practice slowing down and asking: 'Am I describing a scene, or narrating an event?'"

## Common Errors

1. **Defaulting to preterite for everything.** "Cuando fui niño, siempre jugué en el parque" instead of "Cuando era niño, siempre jugaba en el parque." The most common error. Habitual past and background descriptions require the imperfect. -- L1 interference. Ask: "Did this happen once, or was it a general situation?"
2. **Using imperfect for completed sequences.** "Ayer llegaba a casa y comía pizza" instead of "Ayer llegué a casa y comí pizza." A sequence of completed events uses preterite. -- overgeneralization (once imperfect is introduced, some learners overuse it). Ask: "Did this happen step by step, in order?"
3. **Era/fue confusion.** Both mean "it was," but "fue" is a completed assessment and "era" is a background description. "La película fue buena" (I've finished watching -- my verdict) vs "La película era buena" (describing it in the context of a story). -- L1 interference. Both translate to "was" in English.
4. **Wrong tense in interrupted-action pattern.** "Mientras caminé, vi a un perro" instead of "Mientras caminaba, vi a un perro." The ongoing action (walking) is imperfect; the interrupting event (seeing) is preterite. -- developmental. Drill the "mientras + imperfect, de repente + preterite" pattern.
5. **Habitual markers with preterite.** "Siempre comí en ese restaurante" (implies a lifetime habit that ended) instead of "Siempre comía en ese restaurante" (habitual past). Words like siempre, todos los días, cada verano almost always signal imperfect. -- L1 interference. Teach the time-marker associations.

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Present a short story mixing both tenses. Highlight each past-tense verb. Ask the learner: "Some of these verbs use preterite, and some use imperfect. You already know both forms. Can you sort them? Why do you think some are different?"

> Era un viernes por la noche. Hacía frío y llovía un poco. Yo caminaba por el centro cuando vi a un viejo amigo. Me llamó y fuimos a tomar un café. Hablamos durante dos horas. Estaba muy contento de verlo.

Guide the learner to notice: scene-setting verbs (era, hacía, llovía, caminaba, estaba) vs action verbs (vi, llamó, fuimos, hablamos). Don't explain the rule yet -- let them articulate it.

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
"Preterite or imperfect?" decision drill. Present 15 sentences with the verb in infinitive form. The learner chooses the correct tense and conjugates. Include clear cases and ambiguous ones.

1. Cuando (ser) _____ niño, (vivir) _____ en una casa pequeña.
2. Ayer (ir) _____ al supermercado y (comprar) _____ frutas.
3. Todos los veranos (viajar, nosotros) _____ a la playa.
4. (Llover) _____ cuando (salir, yo) _____ de la oficina.
5. El concierto (ser) _____ fantástico.
6. Mi abuelo siempre me (contar) _____ historias.
7. Esa noche (conocer, yo) _____ a mi mejor amigo.
8. (Hacer) _____ mucho calor y todos (tener) _____ sed.

After each answer, discuss the reasoning. The WHY matters more than getting it right.

### Stage 3: Guided Production
Prompt: "Tell me about a trip -- a vacation, a visit to another city, anything. Tell me what happened (the events) AND what it was like (the descriptions). Where did you go? What was the weather like? Who was there? What did you do?"

Listen for natural mixing of both tenses. If the learner uses only preterite, prompt: "What was the weather like that day? What was the city like?" to elicit imperfect descriptions. If they default to imperfect for events, prompt: "And then what happened?"

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Present a sequence of 4-5 images telling a simple story (a day at the beach, a party, a travel mishap). Ask the learner to narrate the story. This naturally requires both tenses: descriptions of what things looked like (imperfect) and what happened next (preterite).

Alternatively: "Tell me a story from your childhood. Something funny that happened." Childhood context naturally triggers both: habitual background (imperfect) and specific memorable events (preterite).

## Dialect Notes
The preterite vs imperfect distinction is universal across all Spanish dialects. There are no regional differences in when to use each tense.

Some dialects show a preference for preterite in certain contexts where others might use present perfect (e.g., "Hoy comí" in Mexico vs "Hoy he comido" in Spain), but this does not affect the preterite/imperfect distinction itself.

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Correctly identifies preterite vs imperfect in decision drills. Can articulate why a sentence uses one tense over the other. Gets 70%+ correct on ambiguous or tricky cases (era/fue, conocí/conocía).
- **Unscaffolded:** Uses both tenses in narration without being prompted. Naturally shifts between scene-setting (imperfect) and events (preterite) when telling a story. Gets the interrupted-action pattern right (mientras + imperfect, de repente + preterite).
- **Integrated:** Maintains aspect distinction while using object pronouns, reflexives, and complex sentence structures. Does not regress to preterite-only when cognitive load increases. Can self-correct when they catch an aspect error.

## Connection Points
This is the B1 gateway concept. Everything in Phase C assumes this distinction is solid. Indirect speech (C-08) requires it explicitly. Subjunctive (C-01) adds another layer of tense choice on top of this one.

Expect months of practice. This is not a concept that is "taught" in one session -- it is introduced, drilled, practiced in conversation, corrected gently over many sessions, and gradually internalized. The decision engine should continue cycling this concept long after initial introduction.

Common regression trigger: when the subjunctive is introduced (Phase C), learners sometimes lose confidence in their preterite/imperfect distinction as they process yet another tense-choice layer. Revisit this concept explicitly if regression is detected.

Build on B-01 (preterite forms), B-02 (irregular preterite forms), and B-03 (imperfect forms). The forms are prerequisites; this concept is about the choice between them.
