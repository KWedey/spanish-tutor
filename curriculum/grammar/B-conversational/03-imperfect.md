# Imperfect Tense

## Overview
The imperfect (pretérito imperfecto) is the second past tense and the easiest to conjugate -- only 3 irregular verbs in the entire language. The forms are simple. The hard part is knowing WHEN to use it instead of the preterite, which is a separate concept (B-04). This lesson focuses purely on the forms and the meaning: ongoing, habitual, or background actions in the past.

## When to Teach
- Phase: B
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular
- L1 interference to preempt: none for forms (the L1 issue is preterite/imperfect CHOICE, addressed in B-04)

## The Pattern

The imperfect has two sets of endings: one for -ar verbs, another shared by -er/-ir verbs. There are only **3 irregular verbs** in the entire imperfect tense.

### -AR Verbs: hablar (to speak)

| Person | Pronoun | Conjugation |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| 1st singular | yo | habl**aba** |
| 2nd singular | tú | habl**abas** |
| 3rd singular | él / ella / usted | habl**aba** |
| 1st plural | nosotros/as | habl**ábamos** |
| 2nd plural | vosotros/as | habl**abais** |
| 3rd plural | ellos / ellas / ustedes | habl**aban** |

> **Key observation:** The yo and él/ella/usted forms are identical (hablaba). Context resolves ambiguity -- same as the present tense pattern with él/ella/usted. The nosotros form has an accent: hablábamos.

### -ER/-IR Verbs: comer (to eat) / vivir (to live)

| Person | Pronoun | Conjugation |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| 1st singular | yo | com**ía** / viv**ía** |
| 2nd singular | tú | com**ías** / viv**ías** |
| 3rd singular | él / ella / usted | com**ía** / viv**ía** |
| 1st plural | nosotros/as | com**íamos** / viv**íamos** |
| 2nd plural | vosotros/as | com**íais** / viv**íais** |
| 3rd plural | ellos / ellas / ustedes | com**ían** / viv**ían** |

> **Key observation:** -ER and -IR share identical imperfect endings, just like the preterite. Every -ía form carries an accent on the i. The nosotros form has an accent: comíamos.

### The Only 3 Irregular Verbs

**Ser (to be):**

| Person | Conjugation |
|--------|-------------|
| yo | **era** |
| tú | **eras** |
| él / ella / usted | **era** |
| nosotros/as | **éramos** |
| vosotros/as | **erais** |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **eran** |

**Ir (to go):**

| Person | Conjugation |
|--------|-------------|
| yo | **iba** |
| tú | **ibas** |
| él / ella / usted | **iba** |
| nosotros/as | **íbamos** |
| vosotros/as | **ibais** |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **iban** |

**Ver (to see):**

| Person | Conjugation |
|--------|-------------|
| yo | **veía** |
| tú | **veías** |
| él / ella / usted | **veía** |
| nosotros/as | **veíamos** |
| vosotros/as | **veíais** |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **veían** |

> **Why ver is irregular:** Regular would be "vía" (dropping the -er and adding -ía). Instead, the "e" is retained: veía. This is the gentlest irregularity in Spanish.

### Time Markers That Signal Imperfect

These words and phrases signal ongoing, habitual, or background actions:

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| siempre | always |
| todos los días | every day |
| cada verano / cada año | every summer / every year |
| de niño / de joven | as a child / as a young person |
| mientras | while |
| generalmente / normalmente | generally / normally |
| a menudo / frecuentemente | often / frequently |
| en aquella época | in that time / back then |

### Five Uses of the Imperfect

1. **Habitual past:** Things you used to do repeatedly. "De niño, **jugaba** en el parque." (As a child, I used to play in the park.)
2. **Background description:** Setting the scene. "**Hacía** sol y los pájaros **cantaban**." (It was sunny and the birds were singing.)
3. **Age, time, and weather in the past:** "**Tenía** diez años." (I was ten years old.) "**Eran** las tres de la tarde." (It was three in the afternoon.)
4. **Ongoing action when interrupted:** "**Caminaba** por la calle cuando empezó a llover." (I was walking down the street when it started to rain.)
5. **Politeness (softened request):** "**Quería** pedirte un favor." (I wanted to ask you a favor.) This is the imperfect used in the present -- a softer way to make a request.

## Examples in Context

1. De niño, **vivía** en una casa pequeña con un jardín grande. (As a child, I lived in a small house with a big garden.)
2. Mi abuela **cocinaba** todos los domingos para la familia. (My grandmother cooked every Sunday for the family.)
3. Cuando **tenía** diez años, **iba** a la escuela caminando. (When I was ten, I walked to school.)
4. Mis hermanos y yo **jugábamos** en la calle hasta que oscurecía. (My siblings and I played in the street until it got dark.)
5. **Era** un día bonito. **Hacía** sol y no **había** nubes. (It was a beautiful day. It was sunny and there were no clouds.)
6. Mi papá siempre **leía** el periódico por la mañana. (My dad always read the newspaper in the morning.)
7. En aquella época, no **teníamos** teléfono celular. (Back then, we didn't have cell phones.)
8. **Quería** preguntarte algo. (I wanted to ask you something.)
9. Mientras yo **estudiaba**, mi hermana **veía** la televisión. (While I was studying, my sister was watching TV.)
10. **Íbamos** a la playa cada verano y **nadábamos** todo el día. (We went to the beach every summer and swam all day.)

## L1 Interference

**English habit:** English uses "used to" or "was doing" for some imperfect meanings, but often the simple past covers both: "I lived there" could mean "I was living there at the time" (imperfect) or "I lived there and then moved" (preterite). English speakers lack the instinct to distinguish these.

**Spanish reality:** The imperfect is a distinct tense with its own forms. For now, focus on learning the forms and associating them with the meaning: ongoing, habitual, background. The choice between imperfect and preterite (B-04) is a separate challenge.

**Contrast pairs:**

| English (ambiguous) | Preterite (completed) | Imperfect (ongoing/habitual) |
|--------------------|-----------------------|------------------------------|
| I lived in Mexico | Viví en México (and left) | Vivía en México (at that time) |
| She worked there | Trabajó allí (for a period, then stopped) | Trabajaba allí (ongoing at that time) |
| We ate at 8 | Comimos a las 8 (that specific time) | Comíamos a las 8 (every day) |

**Preemption script:** "English has one past tense for 'I ate breakfast' and 'I used to eat breakfast every day.' Spanish separates these. The imperfect is for the 'used to' and 'was doing' meanings -- things that were ongoing, repeated, or just part of the background. Don't worry about choosing yet -- just learn these new forms and associate them with habits and descriptions."

## Common Errors

1. **Confusing -aba and -ía endings:** Using "comaba" instead of comía, or "hablía" instead of hablaba. -- developmental. -aba is exclusively for -ar verbs. -ía is exclusively for -er/-ir verbs. Drill the distinction.
2. **Forgetting accent on -íamos:** Writing "comiamos" without the accent, or "viviamos" without it. -- developmental. The nosotros forms of -er/-ir imperfect always carry this accent.
3. **Forgetting accent on -ábamos:** Writing "hablabamos" without the accent. -- developmental. Same pattern: nosotros forms always need the accent.
4. **Overusing ser/ir irregulars:** Trying to make other verbs irregular in the imperfect ("tenía" is actually regular! -- the stem is ten- plus the regular -ía ending). Only ser, ir, and ver are irregular. -- overgeneralization.
5. **Using imperfect for completed single events:** "Ayer comía pizza" instead of "Ayer comí pizza." -- L1 interference. Ayer + single event = preterite. This error is addressed fully in B-04.

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Show two sets of sentences side by side. Set A uses preterite with "ayer" (completed events). Set B uses imperfect with "de niño" and "siempre" (habitual past). Ask: "What feels different about these two groups? Not just the form -- what kind of past are they describing?"

**Set A (Preterite):** Ayer hablé con mi mamá. Anoche comí pizza. La semana pasada visité a mis abuelos.

**Set B (Imperfect):** De niño, hablaba con mis abuelos todos los días. Siempre comíamos pizza los viernes. Visitaba a mis abuelos cada verano.

After the learner identifies the pattern (repeated/habitual vs one-time), show the -aba and -ía paradigms. Point out: "Only 3 irregulars. That's it."

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
Conjugation drills: provide infinitive + subject, learner supplies the imperfect form. Include all three irregulars (ser, ir, ver) mixed with regular verbs. Then sentence completion: provide a sentence with a time marker (siempre, de niño, todos los días) and a blank, learner fills in the imperfect form.

### Stage 3: Guided Production
Prompt: "Tell me about your life when you were 10 years old. Where did you live? What did you do after school? What did your family do on weekends? What did you like to eat?" Provide verb suggestions: vivir, jugar, comer, ir, ver, ser, tener, gustar. Gently recast any preterite slips into imperfect when the meaning is habitual.

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Prompt: "Describe your childhood home and neighborhood. What was it like? What did the house look like? Who lived nearby? What did you and your neighbors do?" This naturally elicits description (era grande, tenía tres cuartos), habitual actions (jugábamos, íbamos), and background states (había un parque, hacía calor en verano).

## Dialect Notes
**Vosotros** forms (hablabais, comíais) follow the standard pattern. Latin American Spanish uses ustedes + 3rd plural (hablaban, comían).

**Voseo:** Imperfect vos forms are identical to tú forms (hablabas, comías). No vos-specific imperfect forms.

Regional vocabulary for childhood topics varies. See `curriculum/dialect-notes.yaml` -- for example, "swimming" might require nadar (universal) but pool vocabulary differs (alberca in Mexico, pileta in Argentina, piscina elsewhere).

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Conjugates -ar and -er/-ir imperfect forms correctly in drills, including the 3 irregulars. Distinguishes -aba from -ía endings without mixing them.
- **Unscaffolded:** Naturally uses imperfect when describing childhood memories, past habits, and background descriptions. Responds to "What was it like when you were young?" entirely in the imperfect.
- **Integrated:** Uses imperfect for description and preterite for events within the same narrative, even if the choice isn't always correct. The willingness to use both tenses, rather than defaulting to one, signals readiness for B-04.

## Connection Points
The imperfect completes the past-tense toolbox. With B-01 (regular preterite), B-02 (irregular preterite), and B-03 (imperfect), the learner has all the forms needed for past narration. The critical next step is B-04 (preterite vs imperfect), which teaches WHEN to use each. The imperfect's simplicity (only 3 irregulars, two predictable ending sets) is both its strength and its trap: the forms are easy, but the CHOICE of when to use imperfect vs preterite is the hardest concept in Phase B. Expect this distinction to take months of practice. Common regression trigger: learners who over-practiced imperfect in isolation may default to it for all past events. Watch for "Ayer cocinaba la cena" when "Ayer cociné la cena" is correct.
