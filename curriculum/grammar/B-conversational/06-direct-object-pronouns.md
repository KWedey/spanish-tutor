# Direct Object Pronouns

## Overview
Direct object pronouns replace the noun that directly receives the action of the verb, eliminating repetition and making speech sound natural. They are essential for fluid conversation -- without them, every sentence repeats the full noun, which no native speaker does. This concept extends the personal 'a' from A-04 and introduces pronoun placement rules that carry directly into indirect object pronouns (B-07).

## When to Teach
- Phase: B
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular, A-04-articles-prepositions (for personal 'a')
- L1 interference to preempt: none specific (English uses object pronouns similarly, but placement differs)

## The Pattern

### The Direct Object Pronouns

The direct object answers "what?" or "whom?" after the verb. The pronoun replaces the full noun:

| Person | Pronoun | Replaces |
|--------|---------|----------|
| 1st singular | me | me (a mí) |
| 2nd singular | te | you (a ti) |
| 3rd singular masc. | lo | him / it (masc. noun) |
| 3rd singular fem. | la | her / it (fem. noun) |
| 1st plural | nos | us (a nosotros/as) |
| 2nd plural | os | you all (a vosotros/as) |
| 3rd plural masc. | los | them (masc. noun) |
| 3rd plural fem. | las | them (fem. noun) |

**Gender matching is critical:** The pronoun must match the gender of the noun it replaces, not the gender of the subject.

- **El libro** es bueno. → **Lo** leo todos los días. (masculine noun = lo)
- **La película** es interesante. → **La** veo esta noche. (feminine noun = la)
- **Los zapatos** están sucios. → **Los** limpio ahora. (masculine plural = los)
- **Las flores** son bonitas. → **Las** compro para mi mamá. (feminine plural = las)

### Pronoun Placement Rules

Direct object pronouns have specific placement depending on the verb form:

**1. Before the conjugated verb (most common position):**
- Veo la casa. → **La** veo. (I see it.)
- Como el pan. → **Lo** como. (I eat it.)
- No quiero el café. → No **lo** quiero. (I don't want it.)

**2. Attached to the infinitive (two valid options):**
- Voy a comer **lo**. → Voy a comer**lo**. (I'm going to eat it.)
- OR: **Lo** voy a comer. (Same meaning -- pronoun moves before the conjugated verb.)
- Both forms are equally correct and common in speech.

**3. Attached to the gerund (note the accent):**
- Estoy comiendo el pan. → Estoy comién**dolo**. (I'm eating it.)
- OR: **Lo** estoy comiendo. (Same meaning.)
- **Accent rule:** Attaching the pronoun adds a syllable, so an accent preserves the original stress: comiéndo**lo**, viéndo**la**, preparándo**los**.

**4. Attached to affirmative commands:**
- Come el pan. → Cóme**lo**. (Eat it.)
- Compra las flores. → Cómpra**las**. (Buy them.)
- **Accent rule applies here too:** The accent is added to maintain the original stress of the verb.

**5. Before negative commands:**
- No comas el pan. → No **lo** comas. (Don't eat it.)
- No compres las flores. → No **las** compres. (Don't buy them.)
- The pronoun goes between "no" and the verb -- never attached to a negative command.

### Personal 'a' Connection

When the direct object is a person, Spanish requires the personal 'a' (from A-04). When replaced by a pronoun, the personal 'a' disappears -- the pronoun absorbs it:

- Veo **a María**. → **La** veo. (I see her.)
- Llamo **a mis padres**. → **Los** llamo. (I call them.)
- Conozco **a tu hermano**. → **Lo** conozco. (I know him.)

The personal 'a' is only needed with the full noun. The pronoun carries the same meaning without it.

## Examples in Context

1. ¿El café? **Lo** tomo todas las mañanas sin azúcar. (The coffee? I drink it every morning without sugar.)
2. Mi hermana necesita las llaves. Voy a dár**selas**... no, primero: Voy a buscar**las**. (My sister needs the keys. I'm going to look for them.)
3. ¿Conoces **a** mi primo? -- Sí, **lo** conozco. Trabaja conmigo. (Do you know my cousin? -- Yes, I know him. He works with me.)
4. La tarea es difícil, pero **la** estoy terminando. (The homework is hard, but I'm finishing it.)
5. Estos libros son muy buenos. **Los** recomiendo. (These books are very good. I recommend them.)
6. ¿Las tortillas? Mi abuela **las** hace a mano todos los días. (The tortillas? My grandmother makes them by hand every day.)
7. **No me llames** tan tarde. -- Está bien, no **te** llamo después de las diez. (Don't call me so late. -- OK, I won't call you after ten.)
8. Ese programa es aburrido. Nunca **lo** veo. (That show is boring. I never watch it.)
9. ¿La carta? Estoy escribién**dola** ahora mismo. (The letter? I'm writing it right now.)
10. ¡Las galletas están listas! ¡Cóme**las** antes de que se enfríen! (The cookies are ready! Eat them before they cool off!)

## L1 Interference

**English placement habit:** English always puts object pronouns after the verb ("I see **it**," "I eat **them**"). Spanish puts them before the conjugated verb or attached to non-finite forms. English speakers instinctively want to place the pronoun after the verb.

**Common L1-driven mistakes:**

| English instinct | Wrong | Correct | Issue |
|-----------------|-------|---------|-------|
| I see it. | Veo lo. | **Lo** veo. | Pronoun after verb |
| I'm eating it. | Estoy comiendo lo. | Estoy comién**dolo** / **Lo** estoy comiendo. | Separated from gerund |
| I want to buy it. | Quiero comprar lo. | Quiero comprar**lo** / **Lo** quiero comprar. | Separated from infinitive |
| I see Maria. | La veo a María. | **La** veo. (pronoun replaces, not doubles) | Keeping both noun and pronoun for direct objects |

**Preemption script:** "In English, you say 'I see it' -- pronoun after the verb. In Spanish, the pronoun jumps in front: 'Lo veo.' Think of it like the pronoun cutting in line -- it always wants to be BEFORE the conjugated verb. The only time it goes after is when it attaches to an infinitive, gerund, or affirmative command."

## Common Errors

1. **Wrong gender on lo/la.** "Lo veo" when referring to "la casa" -- should be "La veo." The pronoun matches the replaced noun's gender, not the subject's gender or the English "it." -- Developmental. Drill with mixed-gender nouns.
2. **Pronoun placed after the conjugated verb.** "Veo lo" instead of "Lo veo." -- L1 interference. English always puts object pronouns after the verb. Practice with the "cutting in line" metaphor.
3. **Forgetting the accent on attached forms.** "Estoy comiendolo" instead of "Estoy comiéndolo." "Comelo" instead of "Cómelo." -- Developmental. The accent preserves the original stress when the extra syllable is added. Teach the rule explicitly and test in writing.
4. **Keeping the full noun alongside the pronoun.** "La veo a María" for direct objects -- unlike indirect objects, standard direct object usage replaces the noun entirely: "La veo." -- Overgeneralization (may occur after learning indirect object doubling in B-07).
5. **Using lo as a catch-all.** Defaulting to "lo" for everything regardless of gender or number -- "Lo como las manzanas" instead of "Las como." -- Developmental. Reinforce that the pronoun must match the replaced noun in both gender AND number.

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Show sentence pairs and ask: "What changed? What disappeared?"
- "Veo **la casa**." → "**La** veo."
- "Como **el pan**." → "**Lo** como."
- "Compro **las flores**." → "**Las** compro."
- "Leo **los libros**." → "**Los** leo."

Guide the learner to notice: the noun is gone, replaced by a small word that moved to before the verb. The small word matches the gender/number of the noun.

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
Q&A drill forcing pronoun use. Tutor asks with the full noun, learner must answer with the pronoun:
- ¿Ves la película? -- Sí, **la** veo. / No, no **la** veo.
- ¿Comes el arroz? -- Sí, **lo** como.
- ¿Compras las revistas? -- No, no **las** compro.
- ¿Conoces a mi hermano? -- Sí, **lo** conozco.

Mix genders and numbers. Include personal 'a' examples. Add negative answers to practice "no lo/la" word order.

### Stage 3: Guided Production
Prompt: "I'm going to ask you about things in your house and your daily life. Answer using a pronoun -- don't repeat the noun." Examples: "¿Dónde pones las llaves cuando llegas a casa?" "¿Con qué frecuencia limpias el baño?" "¿Dónde compras la comida?" If the learner repeats the full noun, recast: "Ah, las pones en la mesa. Bien."

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Prompt: "You're helping a friend pack for a trip. They keep asking you where things are. Answer using pronouns." Example exchange: "¿Dónde está mi pasaporte?" "Lo puse en tu mochila." "¿Y mis camisas?" "Las dejé en la cama." This naturally requires varied pronouns (lo/la/los/las) and different verb forms.

Alternative: "You're at a restaurant describing what you usually order, what you never eat, and what you want to try. Avoid repeating the dish names -- use pronouns."

## Dialect Notes
Direct object pronoun usage is consistent across all major dialects. Two notable variations:

- **Leísmo** (common in central/northern Spain): Using "le" instead of "lo" for masculine persons -- "Le vi" instead of "Lo vi" (I saw him). This is accepted by the RAE for masculine singular persons but is NOT standard in Latin American Spanish. For Mexican Spanish target dialect, always use lo/la.
- **Laísmo/loísmo** (some regions of Spain): Using "la" or "lo" for indirect objects. Not standard anywhere and should be avoided.

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Correctly replaces nouns with matching-gender pronouns in drills. Places pronoun before conjugated verb. Knows both placement options with infinitives and gerunds.
- **Unscaffolded:** Uses pronouns spontaneously in conversation to avoid repeating nouns. Correctly handles personal 'a' replacement ("Veo a Juan" → "Lo veo" without the personal 'a').
- **Integrated:** Maintains correct pronoun placement when combining with other tenses (preterite: "Lo comí," imperfect: "Lo comía") and with progressive and compound forms under conversational pressure.

## Connection Points
This concept directly extends the personal 'a' from A-04 -- learners see how the personal 'a' works with pronouns (it disappears because the pronoun absorbs its function). The pronoun placement rules are identical to those learned with reflexive verbs in B-05, which reduces the learning load.

This is a required prerequisite for B-07 (indirect object pronouns), where both pronoun types combine. The stacking of direct + indirect object pronouns is one of the most mechanically challenging patterns in Phase B, so solid command of direct object placement is essential before proceeding.

When imperatives are introduced in B-09, pronoun attachment to commands (Cómelo, No lo comas) will build directly on the placement rules taught here.

Possible regression: when indirect object pronouns are introduced (B-07), some learners temporarily confuse lo/la (direct) with le/les (indirect). Clarify with the "what?" vs "to whom?" distinction.
