# Imperatives (Commands)

## Overview
Imperatives are how you tell someone to do something — or not to do something. They are essential for giving directions, cooking instructions, advice, and everyday requests like "wait," "look," and "tell me." This concept has its own conjugation patterns and introduces pronoun placement rules that differ between affirmative and negative commands.

## When to Teach
- Phase: B
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular, A-07-present-irregular-common
- L1 interference to preempt: imperative-pronoun-placement (medium severity)

## The Pattern

### Tú Affirmative Commands (Informal "You")

For regular verbs, the tú affirmative command is identical to the 3rd person singular present tense:

| Infinitive | 3rd person present | Tú command |
|-----------|-------------------|------------|
| hablar | habla | **¡Habla!** (Speak!) |
| comer | come | **¡Come!** (Eat!) |
| escribir | escribe | **¡Escribe!** (Write!) |
| pensar | piensa | **¡Piensa!** (Think!) |
| cerrar | cierra | **¡Cierra!** (Close!) |
| dormir | duerme | **¡Duerme!** (Sleep!) |

**The 8 irregular tú commands** (must be memorized):

| Infinitive | Tú command | Mnemonic aid |
|-----------|------------|-------------|
| decir | **di** | Di la verdad. (Tell the truth.) |
| hacer | **haz** | Haz la tarea. (Do the homework.) |
| ir | **ve** | Ve a la tienda. (Go to the store.) |
| poner | **pon** | Pon la mesa. (Set the table.) |
| salir | **sal** | Sal de aquí. (Get out of here.) |
| ser | **sé** | Sé bueno. (Be good.) |
| tener | **ten** | Ten cuidado. (Be careful.) |
| venir | **ven** | Ven aquí. (Come here.) |

### Tú Negative Commands (Informal "You, Don't")

Negative tú commands use a different form — what is technically the present subjunctive tú form. At this stage, teach these as "the negative command form," not as subjunctive (that formal concept comes in C-01).

| Verb type | Formation | Example |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| -ar verbs | no + stem + **-es** | ¡No habl**es**! (Don't speak!) |
| -er verbs | no + stem + **-as** | ¡No com**as**! (Don't eat!) |
| -ir verbs | no + stem + **-as** | ¡No escrib**as**! (Don't write!) |

Notice the "vowel flip": -ar verbs take -es, while -er/-ir verbs take -as.

Common negative commands:
- ¡No **hables** tan rápido! (Don't speak so fast!)
- ¡No **comas** eso! (Don't eat that!)
- ¡No **vayas**! (Don't go!) — irregular, from ir
- ¡No **hagas** eso! (Don't do that!) — irregular, from hacer
- ¡No **digas** nada! (Don't say anything!) — irregular, from decir
- ¡No **seas** tonto! (Don't be silly!) — irregular, from ser
- ¡No **tengas** miedo! (Don't be afraid!) — irregular, from tener
- ¡No **pongas** eso ahí! (Don't put that there!) — irregular, from poner

### Usted Commands (Formal "You")

Usted commands use the same subjunctive-based form for BOTH affirmative and negative:

| Verb type | Formation | Affirmative | Negative |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|----------|
| -ar verbs | stem + **-e** | **¡Hable!** (Speak!) | **¡No hable!** (Don't speak!) |
| -er verbs | stem + **-a** | **¡Coma!** (Eat!) | **¡No coma!** (Don't eat!) |
| -ir verbs | stem + **-a** | **¡Escriba!** (Write!) | **¡No escriba!** (Don't write!) |

The same "vowel flip" applies: -ar takes -e, -er/-ir take -a.

Irregular usted commands:
- ir → **vaya** / no vaya
- ser → **sea** / no sea
- saber → **sepa** / no sepa
- estar → **esté** / no esté
- dar → **dé** / no dé

### Nosotros Commands ("Let's...")

Nosotros commands also use the subjunctive form. Teach them as set phrases:

| Verb | Nosotros command |
|------|-----------------|
| hablar | **¡Hablemos!** (Let's talk!) |
| comer | **¡Comamos!** (Let's eat!) |
| ir | **¡Vamos!** (Let's go!) — note: NOT "vayamos" in affirmative |
| salir | **¡Salgamos!** (Let's leave!) |
| empezar | **¡Empecemos!** (Let's start!) |

Negative: **¡No hablemos** de eso! (Let's not talk about that!)

### Pronoun Placement with Commands

This is where English speakers struggle. The rule is clear but opposite for affirmative vs. negative:

**Affirmative commands — pronoun ATTACHES to the end:**
- **¡Dime!** (Tell me!) — decir + me
- **¡Hazlo!** (Do it!) — hacer + lo
- **¡Dímelo!** (Tell it to me!) — decir + me + lo
- **¡Siéntate!** (Sit down!) — sentar + te
- **¡Cómpralo!** (Buy it!) — comprar + lo

Accent rule: When attaching pronouns, add an accent to maintain original stress: di → dime (no accent needed, still 2 syllables), but diga → dígame (accent needed), compra → cómpralo (accent needed).

**Negative commands — pronoun goes BEFORE the verb:**
- **¡No me digas!** (Don't tell me! / You don't say!)
- **¡No lo hagas!** (Don't do it!)
- **¡No me lo digas!** (Don't tell it to me!)
- **¡No te sientes!** (Don't sit down!)
- **¡No lo compres!** (Don't buy it!)

**Summary pattern:**
- Affirmative: verb**+pronoun** (¡Dímelo!)
- Negative: no + **pronoun** + verb (¡No me lo digas!)

## Examples in Context

1. **¡Ven** aquí, por favor! Necesito tu ayuda. (Come here, please! I need your help.)
2. **¡No toques** eso! Está caliente. (Don't touch that! It's hot.)
3. **Dime** la verdad. ¿Qué pasó anoche? (Tell me the truth. What happened last night?)
4. **¡No me digas!** ¿En serio? (You don't say! Really?) — common conversational expression
5. **Haz** la tarea antes de salir. **No la dejes** para mañana. (Do the homework before going out. Don't leave it for tomorrow.)
6. **Siga** derecho y **doble** a la izquierda en la esquina. (Go straight and turn left at the corner.) — usted, giving directions
7. **¡Comamos** algo! Tengo mucha hambre. (Let's eat something! I'm very hungry.)
8. **Ten** cuidado con el perro — **no te acerques** demasiado. (Be careful with the dog — don't get too close.)
9. **Compra** leche cuando **vayas** al súper, pero **no compres** la marca barata. (Buy milk when you go to the store, but don't buy the cheap brand.)
10. **Ponte** la chaqueta. **No salgas** sin ella — hace mucho frío. (Put on your jacket. Don't go out without it — it's very cold.)

## L1 Interference

**Imperative pronoun placement (medium severity):** English always puts pronouns before the verb, whether the command is affirmative or negative: "Tell **me**," "Don't tell **me**." Spanish splits: attached for affirmative, before for negative.

**Contrast pairs:**

| English | Spanish — correct | Common error |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| Tell me. | **Dime.** | ~~Me di.~~ |
| Don't tell me. | **No me digas.** | ~~No dime.~~ / ~~No digas me.~~ |
| Buy it. | **Cómpralo.** | ~~Lo compra.~~ |
| Don't buy it. | **No lo compres.** | ~~No cómpralo.~~ |
| Sit down. | **Siéntate.** | ~~Te sienta.~~ |

**Preemption script:** "In English, the pronoun always comes in the same place regardless of whether the command is positive or negative: 'Tell me' / 'Don't tell me.' Spanish does something different. For positive commands, the pronoun glues onto the end of the verb: 'Dime.' For negative commands, the pronoun jumps back to before the verb: 'No me digas.' Think of the 'no' as a wall — pronouns can't attach to the verb when 'no' is blocking them."

## Common Errors

1. **Using the infinitive as a command.** "¡Hablar!" instead of "¡Habla!" — L1 interference (English bare infinitive = command). Spanish commands have their own conjugated forms.
2. **Wrong irregular tú form.** "¡Hace!" instead of "¡Haz!" "¡Pone!" instead of "¡Pon!" — Developmental. The 8 irregulars must be memorized. Drill as a set.
3. **Pronoun placement reversal.** "¡Me di!" (pronoun before affirmative command) or "¡No dime!" (pronoun attached to negative command). — L1 interference. Use the "no = wall" metaphor. Drill affirmative/negative pairs side by side.
4. **Using affirmative form for negative commands.** "¡No habla!" instead of "¡No hables!" — Developmental. The negative form uses a different conjugation (the subjunctive form). Drill pairs: habla / no hables, come / no comas.
5. **Missing accent on attached pronouns.** "¡Dimelo!" instead of "¡Dímelo!" "¡Compralo!" instead of "¡Cómpralo!" — Developmental. Same accent rule as gerund attachment (B-08).

## Teaching Sequence

### Stage 1: Noticing
Present a recipe or simple instruction set with commands highlighted:

"Look at this recipe. What do you notice about the verb forms?"
- **Corta** las verduras en pedazos pequeños.
- **Pon** el aceite en la sartén.
- **No pongas** demasiado aceite.
- **Cocina** las verduras por cinco minutos.
- **No las cocines** demasiado.

Guide the learner to notice: these verbs look different from normal conjugations. Some have pronouns glued on (las cocines, las), some are short words (pon, corta). The negative forms look different from the affirmative forms.

### Stage 2: Controlled Practice
Transformation drill — tutor gives a statement, learner converts to a command:

- Tienes que hablar más despacio. → **¡Habla** más despacio!
- No debes comer eso. → **¡No comas** eso!
- Necesitas hacer la tarea. → **¡Haz** la tarea!
- No debes ir solo. → **¡No vayas** solo!

Then pronoun attachment drill:
- Dime + lo → **¡Dímelo!**
- No + me + lo + digas → **¡No me lo digas!**
- Compra + las → **¡Cómpralas!**
- No + las + compres → **¡No las compres!**

### Stage 3: Guided Production
"Give me directions from your house to the nearest store." This naturally elicits commands: "Sal de la casa, dobla a la derecha, sigue derecho, no cruces la calle..." If the learner uses infinitives, recast: "Ah, ¿dices que **doble** a la derecha?"

Follow up with: "Now tell me a recipe for a simple dish you like to cook."

### Stage 4: Communicative Practice
Role-play: "You're babysitting your friend's kids. They're being difficult. Tell them what to do and what NOT to do." This forces natural mixing of affirmative and negative commands with pronoun attachment: "¡Cómete las verduras!" "¡No toques eso!" "¡Dámelo!" "¡No le pegues a tu hermano!"

Alternative: "You're a personal trainer giving instructions during a workout. Tell me what to do, step by step." — "¡Levanta los brazos! ¡No los bajes! ¡Corre más rápido! ¡No pares!"

## Dialect Notes
Command forms show significant dialect variation:

- **Voseo imperatives (Argentina, Uruguay, parts of Central America):** The vos affirmative commands have their own forms — drop the final -r of the infinitive and stress the last syllable: habla**r** → **hablá**, come**r** → **comé**, vivi**r** → **viví**. These are regular for ALL verbs (no irregulars!): decir → **decí**, hacer → **hacé**, venir → **vení**. Teach for recognition if the learner's target dialect is not rioplatense.
- **Usted as default** (Colombia, parts of Central America, some Mexican regions): In these areas, usted commands may be used even in casual contexts where tú commands would be expected elsewhere. If the learner travels to these regions, knowing usted commands is extra valuable.
- **"Órale" and "ándale"** (Mexico): Common informal expressions of encouragement or agreement that function like commands: "¡Órale, hazlo!" (Go on, do it!). Exposure-level only.

See `curriculum/dialect-notes.yaml` for regional preferences.

## Signs of Acquisition
- **Scaffolded:** Correctly forms regular tú affirmative commands (3rd person present). Knows at least 5 of the 8 irregular tú commands without hesitation. Forms negative commands with the correct conjugation. Places pronouns correctly in drills.
- **Unscaffolded:** Uses commands naturally in conversation — giving directions, making suggestions, telling stories. Correctly switches pronoun placement between affirmative and negative without pausing to think.
- **Integrated:** Mixes commands with object pronouns from B-06/B-07 (¡Dámelo! / ¡No me lo des!). Uses informal tú and formal usted commands appropriately based on context. Handles reflexive pronoun attachment (¡Siéntate! / ¡No te sientes!).

## Connection Points
This concept builds on irregular present tense forms from A-07 — many irregular commands derive from irregular present stems (the 8 irregular tú commands are all from verbs already introduced in A-07). The pronoun placement rules extend patterns from B-05 (reflexive attachment) and B-06/B-07 (object pronoun attachment).

The negative command forms use what is formally the present subjunctive conjugation. When C-01 (present subjunctive) is introduced, learners will recognize the forms: "Oh, these are the same endings I use for negative commands!" This creates a natural bridge. Explicitly connect them at that point.

Common regression: when the formal subjunctive is introduced in Phase C, some learners start using subjunctive forms for affirmative tú commands ("¡Hables!" instead of "¡Habla!"). Reinforce that affirmative tú commands keep their own distinct form.
