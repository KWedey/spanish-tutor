Follow the grammar concept template from spec Section 7.1. Read `curriculum/grammar/A-foundation/01-present-regular.md` for style reference. Read `curriculum/l1-interference.yaml` for L1 patterns.

- [ ] **Step 1: Create B-01 preterite regular**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular
- No specific L1 interference for regular forms
- The Pattern: Full conjugation paradigm for -ar (hablé, hablaste, habló, hablamos, hablasteis, hablaron) and -er/-ir (comí, comiste, comió, comimos, comisteis, comieron). Note: -er and -ir share IDENTICAL preterite endings.
- Key point: preterite is for completed, bounded actions in the past ("Yesterday I ate" = "Ayer comí")
- Time markers that trigger preterite: ayer, anoche, la semana pasada, el año pasado, una vez, de repente
- Examples: 8-10 sentences narrating completed past events using -ar/-er/-ir verbs
- Common Errors: accent mark confusion (hablo vs habló), -ar/-er/-ir ending mix-ups, using present tense for past events
- Teaching Sequence: Stage 1 = show 6 sentences about yesterday, ask what changed from present. Stage 2 = conjugation drills. Stage 3 = "Tell me what you did yesterday." Stage 4 = "Describe a memorable day."
- Connection Points: Foundation for B-02 (irregular preterite), B-04 (preterite vs imperfect). First past tense — major milestone.

- [ ] **Step 2: Create B-02 preterite irregular**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: B-01-preterite-regular
- No specific L1 interference
- The Pattern: Group irregular preterites by type:
  1. **U-stem:** tener→tuve, estar→estuve, poder→pude, poner→puse, saber→supe, haber→hube, andar→anduve
  2. **I-stem:** hacer→hice (hizo in 3rd), querer→quise, venir→vine, decir→dije (dijeron)
  3. **J-stem:** traer→traje, decir→dije, producir→produje (3rd pl: -eron NOT -ieron)
  4. **Completely irregular:** ir/ser→fui/fuiste/fue/fuimos/fuisteis/fueron (identical for both verbs — context determines meaning), dar→di/diste/dio/dimos/disteis/dieron
  5. **Spelling changes:** -car→-qué (busqué), -gar→-gué (llegué), -zar→-cé (empecé) — yo form only
- Key point: irregular preterite stems use DIFFERENT endings (no accents on yo/él: tuve, tuvo NOT tuví, tuvó)
- Examples: 10 sentences using a variety of irregular preterites in narrative context
- Common Errors: adding accents to irregular forms, regularizing (hací for hice), ir/ser confusion in preterite
- Teaching Sequence: Teach by stem group, not alphabetically. Most frequent first (ir/ser, hacer, tener, estar).
- Connection Points: Required for B-04 (preterite vs imperfect). High-frequency verbs — these are unavoidable in storytelling.

- [ ] **Step 3: Create B-03 imperfect**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular
- No specific L1 interference for forms (the L1 issue is preterite/imperfect CHOICE, addressed in B-04)
- The Pattern: Two sets of endings:
  - -ar: hablaba, hablabas, hablaba, hablábamos, hablabais, hablaban
  - -er/-ir: comía, comías, comía, comíamos, comíais, comían
  - Only 3 irregulars: ser (era, eras, era, éramos, erais, eran), ir (iba, ibas, iba, íbamos, ibais, iban), ver (veía, veías, veía, veíamos, veíais, veían)
- Key point: imperfect is for ongoing, habitual, or background actions in the past ("When I was young, I played" = "Cuando era joven, jugaba")
- Triggers: siempre, todos los días, cada verano, de niño, mientras, generalmente
- Uses: habitual past, background description, age/time in past, ongoing when interrupted, politeness (quería pedirte...)
- Examples: 8-10 sentences describing childhood routines, past descriptions, ongoing states
- Common Errors: confusing -aba with -ía endings, forgetting accent on -íamos, overusing irregular forms
- Teaching Sequence: Stage 1 = show sentences about childhood routines, contrast with "yesterday I did X" sentences. Ask what feels different. Stage 2 = conjugation drills. Stage 3 = "Tell me about your life when you were 10 years old." Stage 4 = "Describe your childhood home and neighborhood."
- Connection Points: Foundation for B-04 (the distinction). Only 3 irregulars = relatively easy forms. The CHOICE of when to use imperfect vs preterite is the hard part (B-04).

### Task 2: Grammar B-04 and B-05 — Aspect Distinction and Reflexives

**Files:**
- Create: `curriculum/grammar/B-conversational/04-preterite-vs-imperfect.md`
- Create: `curriculum/grammar/B-conversational/05-reflexive-verbs.md`

- [ ] **Step 1: Create B-04 preterite vs imperfect**

This is the HARDEST A2-B1 concept. HIGH-severity L1 interference.

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: B-01, B-02, B-03
- L1 interference: preterite-imperfect-overuse (reference l1-interference.yaml) — HIGH severity
- The Pattern: Not about forms — about MEANING. The same sentence can use either tense with different meaning:
  - "Conocí a María" (I met María — single event) vs "Conocía a María" (I knew María — ongoing state)
  - "Fue difícil" (It was difficult — completed assessment) vs "Era difícil" (It was difficult — background description)
- Framework for choosing:
  - **Preterite:** completed action, specific time, sequence of events, change of state, reaction
  - **Imperfect:** ongoing state, habitual action, background/scene setting, description, simultaneous actions, age/time/weather in past
  - **Together:** "Mientras caminaba (imperfect — ongoing), vi (preterite — interruption) a un perro"
- L1 Interference: "English uses one past tense for both 'I ate' (completed) and 'I was eating' (ongoing). Your instinct will be to use preterite for everything. The imperfect feels unnecessary — but it carries meaning that preterite can't."
- Examples: 10+ sentences showing the contrast, including sentences where BOTH tenses appear together
- Common Errors: defaulting to preterite for everything (most common), using imperfect for completed sequences, era/fue confusion
- Teaching Sequence: Stage 1 = show a short story mixing both tenses, highlight each verb. Ask why some are different. Stage 2 = "Preterite or imperfect?" decision drill with 15 sentences. Stage 3 = "Tell me about a trip — what happened (events) AND what it was like (descriptions)." Stage 4 = Tell a story from a picture sequence.
- Signs of Acquisition: Uses both tenses in narration without prompting. Gets the "interrupted action" pattern right (mientras + imperfect, de repente + preterite).
- Connection Points: This is the B1 gateway. Everything in Phase C assumes this is solid. Expect months of practice. Common regression when subjunctive is introduced.

- [ ] **Step 2: Create B-05 reflexive verbs**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular
- No specific L1 interference (reflexives exist in English but are less common)
- The Pattern:
  - Reflexive pronouns: me, te, se, nos, os, se (placed before conjugated verb)
  - Common reflexive verbs: levantarse, acostarse, ducharse, vestirse, sentarse, sentirse, irse, llamarse, despertarse, dormirse, ponerse, quitarse
  - Some verbs change meaning with reflexive: ir (to go) vs irse (to leave), poner (to put) vs ponerse (to put on/become), dormir (to sleep) vs dormirse (to fall asleep)
  - Pronoun placement: before conjugated verb (me levanto), attached to infinitive (voy a levantarme / me voy a levantar — both valid), attached to gerund (estoy duchándome)
- Examples: 10 sentences describing daily routine using reflexive verbs. Show pronoun placement with infinitives and progressive.
- Common Errors: forgetting the pronoun, wrong pronoun for subject, wrong placement with compound verbs
- Teaching Sequence: Stage 1 = show "Lavo el carro" vs "Me lavo las manos" — what's different? Stage 2 = daily routine drill using reflexives. Stage 3 = "Describe your morning routine from alarm to leaving the house." Stage 4 = "Compare your daily routine with a family member's."
- Connection Points: High-frequency in daily conversation (morning/evening routines). Pronoun placement rules preview B-06/B-07 (object pronouns).

### Task 3: Grammar B-06 and B-07 — Object Pronouns

**Files:**
- Create: `curriculum/grammar/B-conversational/06-direct-object-pronouns.md`
- Create: `curriculum/grammar/B-conversational/07-indirect-object-pronouns.md`

- [ ] **Step 1: Create B-06 direct object pronouns**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular, A-04-articles-prepositions (for personal 'a')
- The Pattern:
  - Direct object pronouns: me, te, lo/la, nos, os, los/las
  - Replace the direct object noun: "Veo la casa" → "La veo"
  - Placement: before conjugated verb (Lo como), attached to infinitive (Voy a comerlo / Lo voy a comer), attached to gerund (Estoy comiéndolo), attached to affirmative command (Cómelo), before negative command (No lo comas)
  - Personal 'a' connection: "Veo a María" → "La veo" (the personal 'a' disappears with the pronoun)
  - Lo/la gender must match the noun replaced: "el libro" → "lo," "la mesa" → "la"
- Examples: 10 sentences showing pronoun replacement with various verb forms
- Common Errors: wrong gender on lo/la, wrong placement, forgetting to add accent on attached forms (comiéndolo)
- Teaching Sequence: Stage 1 = show sentence pairs (full noun → pronoun). Ask what changed. Stage 2 = replacement drills. Stage 3 = Q&A where answers must use pronouns ("¿Ves la película?" "Sí, la veo"). Stage 4 = Conversation avoiding noun repetition.
- Connection Points: Directly extends personal 'a' from A-04. Required for B-07 (indirect pronouns). Pronoun stacking (B-07) is a major challenge.

- [ ] **Step 2: Create B-07 indirect object pronouns**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: B-06-direct-object-pronouns
- The Pattern:
  - Indirect object pronouns: me, te, le, nos, os, les (to/for whom)
  - "Doy el libro a María" → "Le doy el libro"
  - Same placement rules as direct objects
  - Redundant "le/les": common and correct to double ("Le doy el libro a María")
  - **Stacking (the hard part):** When both direct and indirect appear: indirect FIRST, then direct. "Le lo doy" → NO, becomes "Se lo doy" (le/les change to se before lo/la/los/las)
  - Full stacking pattern: se + lo/la/los/las + verb
- Examples: 10 sentences showing indirect objects, then stacked combinations
- Common Errors: wrong stacking order, forgetting le→se change, omitting redundant le/les
- Teaching Sequence: Stage 1 = indirect only (no stacking). Stage 2 = introduce stacking with "se lo" pattern. Stage 3 = "Tell me who you gave gifts to last Christmas — use pronouns." Stage 4 = Role-play giving directions where you constantly refer back to objects/people.
- Connection Points: Builds on A-06 gustar (already uses indirect object pronouns me/te/le). Stacking is one of the more mechanical challenges — requires practice but is rule-governed.

- [ ] **Step 1: Create B-08 estar + gerund (progressive)**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular, A-02-ser-vs-estar
- L1 interference: progressive-overuse (reference l1-interference.yaml) — medium severity
- The Pattern:
  - estar (conjugated) + gerund (-ando for -ar, -iendo for -er/-ir)
  - Gerund formation: hablar→hablando, comer→comiendo, vivir→viviendo
  - Irregular gerunds: leer→leyendo, dormir→durmiendo, pedir→pidiendo, ir→yendo, decir→diciendo
  - Used for actions happening RIGHT NOW, not habitual actions
- L1 Interference: "English uses 'I'm eating' for both 'right now' and 'these days.' In Spanish, 'Estoy comiendo' is ONLY for right now. For 'these days I'm eating more,' use present simple: 'Como más.'"
- Examples: 8-10 sentences with contrast between progressive (right now) and present simple (habitual)
- Common Errors: overusing progressive for habitual actions, irregular gerund forms, pronoun placement with gerund (accent: comiéndolo)
- Connection Points: Reinforces estar. Pronoun attachment on gerunds previewed in B-05 (reflexives) and B-06/B-07.

- [ ] **Step 2: Create B-09 imperatives**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular, A-07-present-irregular-common
- L1 interference: imperative-pronoun-placement (reference l1-interference.yaml) — medium severity
- The Pattern:
  - **Tú affirmative:** Same as 3rd person present (habla, come, vive). Irregulars: di, haz, ve, pon, sal, sé, ten, ven
  - **Tú negative:** no + present subjunctive tú form (no hables, no comas, no vivas)
  - **Usted:** present subjunctive usted form — affirmative AND negative (hable/no hable, coma/no coma)
  - Pronoun placement: attached to affirmative (¡Dímelo!), before negative (¡No me lo digas!)
  - Nosotros commands: present subjunctive (¡Hablemos! ¡Comamos!)
- Note: negative tú and all usted/nosotros commands use subjunctive forms — learner hasn't formally learned subjunctive yet. Teach these as command forms, not as "subjunctive." The formal subjunctive comes in Phase C.
- Examples: 10 sentences mixing affirmative/negative, tú/usted, with pronoun placement
- Common Errors: using infinitive as command (*hablar instead of habla), wrong irregulars, pronoun placement reversal
- Teaching Sequence: Stage 1 = tú affirmative only (easiest). Stage 2 = add irregulars. Stage 3 = add negative. Stage 4 = "Give me directions to your house" / "Tell me a recipe step by step."
- Dialect Notes: voseo imperative forms (Argentina) — hablá, comé, viví. Teach for recognition if not target dialect.

- [ ] **Step 3: Create B-10 comparatives and superlatives**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-03-gender-agreement
- L1 interference: comparative-de-vs-que (reference l1-interference.yaml) — low severity
- The Pattern:
  - **Comparatives:** más + adj + que (más grande que), menos + adj + que, tan + adj + como (as...as)
  - **Irregulars:** bueno→mejor (NOT más bueno), malo→peor, grande→mayor (older/greater), pequeño→menor (younger/lesser)
  - **De vs que:** "más de" + number (Tengo más de cinco libros), "más que" + noun/clause (Es más alto que su hermano)
  - **Superlatives:** el/la/los/las + más/menos + adj (+ de group): "Es la más alta de la clase"
  - **Absolute superlative:** -ísimo/a (grandísimo, facilísimo, riquísimo)
- Examples: 10 sentences comparing people, places, things. Include de vs que contrast.
- Common Errors: "más mejor" (double comparative), de vs que confusion, forgetting agreement on superlative article
- Teaching Sequence: Stage 1 = show comparison sentences, ask what pattern. Stage 2 = comparison drills. Stage 3 = "Compare two cities you know." Stage 4 = "Who is the most [X] person in your family?"

- [ ] **Step 4: Create B-11 future with ir + a**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-01-present-regular
- No specific L1 interference (structure is similar to English "going to")
- The Pattern:
  - ir (conjugated present) + a + infinitive
  - "Voy a comer" = I'm going to eat
  - All persons: voy a, vas a, va a, vamos a, vais a, van a + infinitive
  - This is the MOST COMMON way to express future in spoken Spanish (more common than formal future tense, which is Phase C)
  - Time markers: mañana, esta noche, el próximo año, la semana que viene, luego, después
- Examples: 8-10 sentences about plans and intentions
- Common Errors: forgetting "a" (voy comer), conjugating the second verb (voy a como), confusing with "ir" meaning "to go"
- Teaching Sequence: Stage 1 = "What are you going to do this weekend?" — tutor uses the structure naturally, learner notices. Stage 2 = transformation drill (present → future: "Como pizza" → "Voy a comer pizza"). Stage 3 = "Plan our next vacation." Stage 4 = "What is your family going to do this summer?"
- Connection Points: Easy win after the hard B-04/B-06/B-07 concepts. Builds confidence. The formal future tense (C-03) adds another option later.

- [ ] **Step 1: Create rr-trill.md**

Phase B. The alveolar trill — often the hardest sound for English speakers.
- IPA: /r/ (trill)
- Articulation: tongue tip vibrates against alveolar ridge. NOT the English R (retroflex).
- When it occurs: written "rr" between vowels (perro, carro), word-initial "r" (rojo, rápido), after "n/l/s" (Enrique, alrededor)
- Minimal pairs: pero/perro, caro/carro, coro/corro, para/parra
- Practice tip: Start with "butter" in American English (the quick 'd' tap) — that's the single R. The trill is that motion repeated rapidly.
- Assignment: Speechling recordings of 10 rr words, Forvo listening. Daily 2-minute trill practice.
- Signs of acquisition: Speechling coach reports acceptable trill. Whisper correctly transcribes rr words.

- [ ] **Step 2: Create r-single.md**

Phase B. The single tap/flap R.
- IPA: /ɾ/ (tap)
- Articulation: Single quick tap of tongue against alveolar ridge. Similar to American English "butter" or "ladder" middle consonant.
- When it occurs: written "r" between vowels (pero, cara), after consonant (tres, precio), at end of syllable (hablar, comer)
- Minimal pairs with rr: pero/perro, caro/carro, cero/cerro
- Common English error: using English retroflex R instead of tap
- Assignment: Speechling recordings contrasting r/rr words.

- [ ] **Step 3: Create b-v-equivalence.md**

Phase B. B and V are the SAME SOUND in Spanish.
- IPA: /b/ (after pause or nasal) or /β/ (between vowels — soft, fricative)
- Key point: there is NO difference between B and V pronunciation. "Vaca" and "baca" sound identical.
- Between vowels: both become a soft sound where lips don't fully close (bilabial fricative /β/)
- Common English error: pronouncing V with teeth on lip (labiodental, like English V)
- Minimal pairs: N/A — B and V are identical
- Practice: Words with V that English speakers mispronounce: vivir, volver, verde, viaje
- Assignment: Speechling recordings focusing on V words. Listen for lack of teeth-on-lip.

- [ ] **Step 4: Create d-soft.md**

Phase B. The soft/fricative D between vowels.
- IPA: /d/ (after pause or nasal) or /ð/ (between vowels — like English "this")
- Between vowels: D softens to a "th" sound (like English "the"): nada = "na-tha," todo = "to-tho," cansado = "cansa-tho"
- Word-initial or after n: hard D (like English): "donde," "anda"
- Common English error: always using hard D (sounds robotic/foreign)
- Minimal pairs: Not about different meanings — about sounding natural
- Practice: nada, todo, cansado, verdad, ciudad, dedo
- Assignment: Speechling with focus on soft D between vowels.

- [ ] **Step 5: Create linking.md**

Phase B. Connected speech — words flow together.
- Key patterns:
  - Vowel + vowel across words: "mi amigo" sounds like "mia-migo" (vowels merge)
  - Consonant + vowel: "los amigos" sounds like "lo-sa-mi-gos"
  - Same vowels merge: "de España" → "des-pa-ña"
  - S before vowel links: "es una" → "e-su-na"
- Why it matters: native speakers don't pause between words. Lack of linking makes speech choppy.
- Common English error: separating each word with a pause
- Practice: Short phrases, gradually longer. Listen and shadow native speakers.
- Assignment: Speechling with multi-word phrases. Dreaming Spanish — notice linking patterns.
