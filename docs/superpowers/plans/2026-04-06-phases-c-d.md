### Task 1: Grammar C-01, C-02 — Subjunctive Introduction

**Files:**
- Create: `curriculum/grammar/C-intermediate/01-present-subjunctive.md`
- Create: `curriculum/grammar/C-intermediate/02-subjunctive-triggers.md`

Follow grammar template (spec Section 7.1). Read `curriculum/grammar/B-conversational/04-preterite-vs-imperfect.md` for style on a high-difficulty concept. Read `curriculum/l1-interference.yaml` for `subjunctive-avoidance`.

- [ ] **Step 1: Create C-01 present subjunctive (forms)**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-01, A-07 (regular and irregular present)
- L1 interference: subjunctive-avoidance (HIGH severity) — "English rarely uses subjunctive. Your instinct will be to use indicative everywhere. The subjunctive isn't optional — it's required in specific contexts and using indicative sounds wrong to native speakers."
- The Pattern: A NEW MOOD, not a new tense. Formation:
  - -ar verbs → -e endings (hable, hables, hable, hablemos, habléis, hablen) — swap to -er endings
  - -er/-ir verbs → -a endings (coma, comas, coma, comamos, comáis, coman) — swap to -ar endings
  - Mnemonic: "opposite vowel" — -ar verbs take -e, -er/-ir verbs take -a
  - Stem-changing verbs: same changes (quiera, pueda, duerma) plus nosotros changes for -ir (sintamos, durmamos)
  - Irregular subjunctive: sea, vaya, haya, sepa, dé, esté (DISHES mnemonic: dar, ir, ser, haber, estar, saber)
- Key point: Don't explain ALL uses yet — that's C-02. This file teaches the FORMS and gives a taste of when it's used.
- Brief introduction to usage: after expressions of desire (quiero que...), emotion (me alegra que...), doubt (dudo que...), and impersonal expressions (es importante que...)
- Examples: 10 sentences showing subjunctive forms in context. All should include "que" clause structure.
- Common Errors: using indicative instead of subjunctive (*quiero que vienes → quiero que vengas), wrong "opposite vowel," irregular form errors
- Teaching: Stage 1 = show indicative/subjunctive pairs, ask what's different about the verb forms. Stage 2 = conjugation drills. Stage 3 = "Tell me what you want other people to do." Stage 4 = "Give me advice about learning Spanish" (es importante que..., recomiendo que...).
- Signs of Acquisition: Uses subjunctive forms after triggers without hesitation. Gets irregular forms right.
- Connection: THE B2 gateway concept. Everything in C-02 through C-09 and Phase D builds on this. Expect it to take weeks to feel natural.

- [ ] **Step 2: Create C-02 subjunctive triggers**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: C-01
- No additional L1 interference (the avoidance pattern was addressed in C-01)
- The Pattern: WHEN to use subjunctive. Organized by category:
  - **WEIRDO** framework (or similar mnemonic): Wishes, Emotions, Impersonal expressions, Recommendations, Doubt/Denial, Ojalá
  - **Wishes/Desires:** querer que, desear que, esperar que, preferir que, pedir que, necesitar que
  - **Emotions:** alegrarse de que, sentir que, temer que, sorprender que, molestar que, gustar que
  - **Impersonal:** es importante que, es necesario que, es posible que, es mejor que, es una lástima que
  - **Recommendations:** recomendar que, sugerir que, aconsejar que, insistir en que
  - **Doubt/Denial:** dudar que, no creer que, no pensar que, negar que, no es cierto que
  - **Ojalá:** always subjunctive (ojalá que llueva)
  - **Key rule:** subjunctive requires TWO different subjects. "Quiero comer" (same subject → infinitive) vs "Quiero que tú comas" (different subjects → subjunctive)
- When NOT to use subjunctive: statements of fact (creo que viene — indicative), certainty (es cierto que es verdad), after cuando/si in present (cuando llueve, voy...)
- Examples: 12+ sentences across all trigger categories
- Common Errors: using subjunctive when same subject (should be infinitive), using indicative after negative belief verbs, forgetting subjunctive after impersonal expressions
- Teaching: Stage 1 = sort sentences into "triggers subjunctive" / "doesn't trigger." Stage 2 = transformation drill (indicative → subjunctive with trigger). Stage 3 = "Give advice to a friend about their problem." Stage 4 = debate format: "I think X... I doubt that Y... It's important that Z..."
- Connection: Unlocks C-07 (relative clauses with subjunctive), C-08 (indirect speech with subjunctive), and all Phase D subjunctive work.

**Files:**

- [ ] **Step 1: Create C-03 formal future tense**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-01
- No L1 interference
- The Pattern: infinitive + endings (é, ás, á, emos, éis, án). Same endings for ALL verb groups.
  - hablaré, hablarás, hablará, hablaremos, hablaréis, hablarán
  - comeré, comerás... viviré, vivirás...
  - Irregular stems (same 12 as conditional): tener→tendr-, salir→saldr-, poder→podr-, saber→sabr-, haber→habr-, poner→pondr-, venir→vendr-, decir→dir-, hacer→har-, querer→querr-, caber→cabr-, valer→valdr-
- Usage: predictions (Mañana lloverá), promises (Te llamaré), formal plans, probability in present (Serán las tres = It's probably 3:00)
- Contrast with ir+a: ir+a is more common in speech for plans. Formal future is more common in writing, news, formal speech, and for probability.
- Common Errors: wrong irregular stems, confusing with conditional, using for immediate plans (ir+a is better)
- Teaching: Stage 1 = "What will the world be like in 50 years?" (natural future use). Stage 2 = conjugation drill. Stage 3 = "Make 5 predictions about your life." Stage 4 = "Debate: will AI replace teachers?"

- [ ] **Step 2: Create C-04 conditional**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: C-03 (same stems)
- No L1 interference (English conditional with "would" maps well)
- The Pattern: infinitive + endings (ía, ías, ía, íamos, íais, ían). Same irregular stems as future.
  - hablaría, hablarías, hablaría, hablaríamos, hablaríais, hablarían
  - Irregulars: tendría, saldría, podría, sabría, habría, pondría, vendría, diría, haría, querría
- Usage: hypothetical (Sería mejor = It would be better), polite requests (¿Podrías ayudarme?), advice (Yo que tú, estudiaría más), reported future in past (Dijo que vendría)
- Contrast with imperfect: both can express "used to" in some contexts, but conditional is for hypothetical
- Common Errors: confusing conditional endings with imperfect -ía (comía vs comería), wrong irregular stems
- Teaching: Stage 1 = "What would you do with a million dollars?" Stage 2 = conjugation drill (leverage shared stems with future). Stage 3 = "Give me advice using conditional." Stage 4 = "If you could live anywhere, where would you live and why?"
- Connection: Required for D-02 (si clauses). The conditional + past subjunctive combination is the core of hypothetical speech.

- [ ] **Step 3: Create C-05 por vs para**

**Key content requirements:**
- Prerequisites: A-04 (basic preposition knowledge)
- No specific L1 interference entry, but this IS notoriously difficult because English uses one word ("for") where Spanish uses two
- The Pattern:
  - **PARA:** destination (Voy para Madrid), purpose/goal (Estudio para aprender), recipient (Es para ti), deadline (Para el viernes), comparison (Para un niño, habla bien), opinion (Para mí, es fácil)
  - **POR:** cause/reason (Lo hice por amor), exchange (Pagué $10 por el libro), duration (Estudié por 2 horas), movement through (Caminé por el parque), per/each (Tres veces por semana), means (Por teléfono), on behalf of (Hablo por mi hermano), agent in passive (Fue escrito por Cervantes)
  - Mnemonics: PARA = Purpose, And recipient, Result, Ahead (destination/deadline). POR = Payment, Origin/cause, Route, Duration.
- Fixed expressions: por favor, por ejemplo, por supuesto, por fin, por eso, para siempre, para nada, para colmo
- Examples: 12+ sentences showing the contrast. Include tricky cases where both are possible with different meanings.
- Common Errors: using por for purpose (*Estudio por aprender), using para for cause (*Lo hice para amor), para for duration (*Viajé para dos horas)
- Teaching: Stage 1 = sort 12 sentences into por/para groups, look for patterns. Stage 2 = fill-in drills. Stage 3 = describe a trip using both. Stage 4 = "Explain why you do the things you do" (mixes purpose and cause).

- [ ] **Step 1: Create C-06 present perfect and compound tenses**

- Prerequisites: B-01
- haber (he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han) + past participle (-ado/-ido). Irregular participles: dicho, hecho, escrito, visto, puesto, roto, abierto, vuelto, muerto, cubierto
- Present perfect: He comido (I have eaten). Pluperfect: Había comido (I had eaten). Future perfect: Habré comido. Conditional perfect: Habría comido.
- Dialect note: Spain uses present perfect for recent past ("He comido hoy"), Latin America uses preterite ("Comí hoy").
- Focus on present perfect and pluperfect — the other compounds are lower frequency.

- [ ] **Step 2: Create C-07 relative clauses**

- Prerequisites: C-01
- que (most common), quien/quienes (for people, after preposition), donde (for places), lo que (what/that which), el/la/los/las que (the one that), cuyo/a/os/as (whose)
- que vs quien: "La persona que vi" vs "La persona con quien hablé" (quien after preposition)
- Subjunctive in relative clauses: when the antecedent is uncertain/nonexistent: "Busco un libro que sea interesante" (subjunctive — haven't found it yet) vs "Tengo un libro que es interesante" (indicative — it exists)

- [ ] **Step 3: Create C-08 indirect speech**

- Prerequisites: C-01, C-02, B-04
- L1 interference: indirect-speech-subjunctive (reference l1-interference.yaml)
- Reporting what someone said: "Dijo que venía" (He said he was coming) — tense backshift
- Tense shift rules: present→imperfect, preterite→pluperfect, future→conditional
- Subjunctive in indirect speech: when reporting commands/requests/wishes: "Me pidió que viniera" (He asked me to come — subjunctive required)
- Reporting questions: "Me preguntó dónde vivía" (accent marks retained)

- [ ] **Step 4: Create C-09 diminutives and augmentatives**

- Prerequisites: A-03
- L1 interference: diminutive-underuse (reference l1-interference.yaml)
- Diminutives: -ito/-ita (most common), -cito/-cita (after consonant or -e: cafecito, pobrecito), -illo/-illa (less affectionate, sometimes pejorative)
- Augmentatives: -ote/-ota (big), -azo/-aza (big/impressive), -ón/-ona (big, sometimes pejorative)
- Usage: affection (abuelita, hijito), smallness (casita, perrito), softening (ahorita = right now but softer), intensity (grandote, cochazo)
- Cultural: ESSENTIAL for natural Mexican Spanish. Avoiding diminutives sounds cold/formal.
- Dialect: -ico/-ica (Costa Rica, Colombia), -illo/-illa (Spain), -ito/-ita (universal)
- Spelling changes: z→c before -ito (pedazo→pedacito), ending drops before suffix

- [ ] **Step 1: Create D-01 past subjunctive (imperfect subjunctive)**

- Prerequisites: C-01, B-03
- Two forms: -ra (more common) and -se (literary/formal). Based on 3rd person plural preterite stem: hablaron→habla- → hablara/hablase, comieron→comie- → comiera/comiese
- Irregulars follow preterite: tuvieron→tuvie- → tuviera, fueran→fue- → fuera
- Usage: after past-tense triggers (Quería que vinieras), wishes (Ojalá tuviera más tiempo), polite alternatives (Quisiera un café)

- [ ] **Step 2: Create D-02 si clauses (if...then)**

- Prerequisites: C-04, D-01
- Three types:
  1. Real/likely: Si + present indicative, present/future/command (Si llueve, no voy)
  2. Hypothetical present: Si + past subjunctive, conditional (Si tuviera dinero, viajaría)
  3. Hypothetical past: Si + pluperfect subjunctive, conditional perfect (Si hubiera sabido, habría ido)
- Common errors: using conditional in the si clause (*Si tendría...), mixing types
- Teaching: "If you won the lottery..." → "If you had won..." progression

- [ ] **Step 3: Create D-03 subjunctive across all tenses**

- Prerequisites: D-01, C-06
- Integration concept: present subjunctive, imperfect subjunctive, present perfect subjunctive (haya hablado), pluperfect subjunctive (hubiera hablado)
- Sequence of tenses: main clause tense determines subjunctive tense
  - Present/future main → present subjunctive (Quiero que vengas)
  - Past main → imperfect subjunctive (Quería que vinieras)
  - Present main + completed action → present perfect subjunctive (Me alegro de que hayas venido)
  - Past main + earlier action → pluperfect subjunctive (Dudaba que hubiera llegado)

- [ ] **Step 4: Create D-04 passive voice and se constructions**

- Prerequisites: B-01
- True passive: ser + past participle + por (El libro fue escrito por Cervantes)
- Passive se (more common): Se habla español aquí, Se venden coches
- Impersonal se: Se dice que..., Se puede ver que...
- Se accidental: Se me cayó el vaso (the glass fell on me — removes blame)
- Multiple uses of "se" is one of the most complex areas of Spanish

- [ ] **Step 5: Create D-05 register shifting**

- Prerequisites: None (but assumes broad grammar knowledge)
- Tú/usted/vos: when to use each, social context, regional norms
- Formal vs informal register: vocabulary shifts (casa→domicilio, comer→cenar/almorzar), verb forms, pronouns
- Written register: academic, professional, creative
- Teaching: role-play shifting between registers (talking to a friend vs a job interviewer vs an elderly neighbor)
- Cultural: register expectations vary dramatically by country. Colombia is more formal, Argentina more informal.
- Connection: grammar forms in D-05, social judgment in cultural_awareness.register_shifting

- [ ] **Step 6: Create D-06 nuanced connectors**

- Prerequisites: None (assumes broad grammar knowledge)
- Connectors that elevate speech from functional to fluent:
  - Contrast: sin embargo, no obstante, a pesar de (que), aunque, por otro lado, en cambio
  - Cause/result: por lo tanto, por consiguiente, de ahí que (+ subjunctive!), como consecuencia, debido a que
  - Addition: además, es más, asimismo, incluso, ni siquiera
  - Concession: si bien, aun cuando, por más que (+ subjunctive)
  - Sequence: en primer lugar, a continuación, por último, en definitiva, en resumen
- Teaching: transform simple sentences into complex ones using connectors. "It rained. We went." → "A pesar de que llovió, fuimos."

- [ ] **Step 1: Create all 5 Phase C pronunciation guides**

**g-soft.md:** Phase C. Soft g before e/i (/x/ or /h/ depending on dialect). Same sound as j. gente, girar, general, Argentina. Minimal pairs: gato (hard) vs gente (soft). Common error: English hard G in "gente."

**j-sound.md:** Phase C. Jota /x/ (or /h/ in Caribbean). jugar, trabajo, jefe, reloj. Varies by region: uvular in Spain (stronger), glottal in Caribbean (weaker/aspirated). Minimal pairs: hard to find true minimal pairs — focus on production. Common error: English "h" is too weak for Castilian jota.

**ny-sound.md:** Phase C. Palatal nasal /ɲ/. español, niño, año, señor, otoño, mañana. Written as ñ. No English equivalent — closest is "ny" in "canyon." Minimal pairs: ano/año, una/uña, pena/peña. Common error: pronouncing as "n" + "y" separately instead of single palatal sound.

**ll-y-sound.md:** Phase C. Historically distinct sounds now merged in most dialects (yeísmo). Standard: both = /ʝ/ (like English "yes"). Argentine/Uruguayan: both = /ʃ/ (like English "sh"). Minimal pairs: historically pollo/poyo but now homophonous. Regional variation is the main teaching point. Common error: English "l" + "l" for ll.

**intonation.md:** Phase C. Suprasegmental — patterns across phrases, not individual sounds.
- Statements: pitch falls at the end
- Yes/no questions: pitch rises at the end
- Information questions (with question word): pitch falls at the end (like statements)
- Exclamations: wider pitch range
- Enumeration: rise on each item, fall on the last
